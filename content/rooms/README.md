# Rooms

## Purpose

Room templates, one folder per biome. A room is a tile-based arena where enemies are fought (the ones the run's map puts in it: run generation RG3; no waves, boss OB2a), a reward is picked or a story event happens.

**Built in M3** (Run structure; see docs/ROADMAP.md). A small M1 base class already exists (`core/base/room_base.gd`, `RoomBase`); M3 extends it. The example to copy is the M1 crypt arena (see "Example to copy"); M3 adds more rooms.

## Contract

- A room is a `.tscn` scene with tiles from the shared tileset (M1 uses repeated `Sprite2D` tiles; a `TileMapLayer` is welcome once a tileset resource exists), plus a `.tres` on the room base class (`RoomBase`: `id`, `scene` and rules over the whole room, `stand_up_after`, `ambient`, the colour and mote overrides; M3 extends it).
- **The scene is the one place a position is written** (props W8a/W8c, docs/milestones/props.md, "W8c: a room foundation"), so a room is edited by hand in the Godot editor and what you move is what the game reads. The game builds a room's layout once a run (`RoomBase.build()`: its scene; a generated room, M3, from pieces by the run's seeded chance) and reads what is where from that instance, the one it shows (`RoomPlaces.read`). Everything is found **at any depth, in the tree's order**, so a piece of scene carries its own:
  - spawns and doors: `Marker2D`s under a node named `PlayerSpawns` (by seat), `EnemySpawns`, `Doors` or `JoinerSpawns` (by seat; where a seat that joins the room under way appears, the hub's beside its altar; none: the player spawn farthest from danger);
  - **what stands in it: a `RoomPlacement`** (core/base), a point holding the data of what stands there. Its kinds are editor scripts in tools/: `PropStandIn` (Add Child Node, "PropStandIn"; pick its `prop`: the editor draws the prop exactly as the game will, in any `state`, its hurt shape red, its ground cyan, where it is stood on yellow) `EnemyStandIn` (pick its `enemy`: the editor shows its scene; the training and shooting dummies) and `ItemStandIn` (items I6: pick its `item`, a premade staff of content/items/ or a card; no body stands for it: Flow hands the room's items to the Items area, `room_items_placed`, which lays one copy each). A new kind of placed thing is a new placement class and data class, nothing in the reader;
  - **its zones: a `RoomZone`** (core/base, a `ReferenceRect` drawn in the editor only, its `zone_name` written in its corner; move and size it by its handles), for a place that is no thing (the hub's `prop_yard`), or the place of a prop the room must not name, a zone named for its role (the hub's `start_lectern`: the game stands the registry's prop of that role at its middle, so deleting that prop's folder leaves the room whole; run generation RG7). A zone means what the game makes of its name (Flow), never anything in `RoomBase`. **A thing that means something is a self-contained prop instead** (owner, 2026-11-11): a prop says what it is (`PropBase.role`) and where it is stood on (`PropBase.area_size`), so moving it moves everything it means.
  - **Places are whole translations:** a rotated, skewed or scaled node on the way to a place is a problem (`RoomPlaces.problems()`; the engine's trigonometry differs between platforms, the game's places may not).
  - **its ground cover's fields: a `CoverField`** (core/base, ground cover GC2, docs/milestones/ground-cover.md): a zone with data, a cover kind (`kind`, a `content/cover` id) and a `density` over its rectangle; it needs no name. Fields lie over the biome's own fill (each cover kind whose `biomes` names the room's `biome` fills its open floor by itself), in the tree's order, each deciding its own cells: its kind at its density, or none for a field of no kind (keep paths clear with one). A room's `.tres` turns the fill off with `cover_fill = false`. Where the cover grows is worked out from the layout alone (`CoverLayout.of`: its walls, doors, spawns, placements and fields), never kept: nothing covers a wall's cell or a door's, spawn's or placement's.
  The game takes every placement and zone out of the room it shows (`RoomPlaces.strip`) and stands the real bodies there (`RoomContents`, gameplay/flow). `tests/content/rooms/test_room_places.gd` checks every room: its places are sound, and a prop's stand-in draws what the game's body draws, state by state. The placements and zones are the only scripts a room's scene holds (editor scripts; never game code).
- **Decoration** (W8c conventions, built when wanted): static art is the scene's sprites and tiles, with `mote_source` and `light_spot` nodes (as today). Decor that only reacts to bodies (grass swaying as a wizard walks through) is Feel's, never the simulation's: a decor field node in a group, scattered by Feel into one batch and swayed by a shader fed the bodies near it. Decor that changes the game (grass that burns, slows, is trampled) is one simulation grid of cells, never an actor a tuft, placed as a `RoomZone` with data of its own.
- Rooms are discovered by folder through `core/registry`: put it in its biome's folder, no list to edit.
- **As a run's template** (run generation RG1, docs/milestones/run-generation.md): its `biome` (`&"crypt"`) and the room kinds it can host (`kinds`: `fight`, `boss`; content/room_kinds), so a run's map of that biome may pick it for a room of that kind. A room with no kinds (the hub, the sandbox) is offered for no run; one with kinds must name its biome (`RoomBase.template_problems`, checked by `tests/content/runs/test_runs.gd`). The crypt arena hosts `fight`.
- **Its exits** (run generation RG4): a room of a run's map leads on by its exits, props of the role `room_exit` (`content/props/room_exit`, a `PropStandIn` each) placed where the party leaves, on the floor clear of walls; a template stands only for a room with no more links than it has exits (`RoomBase.exit_count`), and a kind that leads on needs a template with exits for the most ways a room may lead (`MapRules.exit_problems`: 2 for the crypt run). Dark until the room is cleared, then one lit a way, left to right; the last room's (the boss's) need none. The crypt arena has two, either side of its top door.
- Space for four: the room must be playable and readable with 4 players, with room to dodge and no spot where a player can get stuck.
- Valid doors and spawns: the required doors, spawn points for 4 players, and enemy spawn points that are reachable and not inside walls. Validators for this arrive when M3 extends the room base class.
- Rooms hold layout only. Enemies come from `content/enemies/` through the run's map (gameplay/run: by the room's `biome` and the kind it hosts) and the spawn points (a room that hosts a kind has enemy spawns and a player spawn a seat: `tests/content/rooms/test_room_templates.gd`); do not reference other areas directly. A room that stands dummies or props holds their placements (above), whose data the game reads.
- Tiles come from the shared tilesets in `art/tilesets/`; do not add new art here.
- What its walls shed when struck (Feel's chips) takes the colours of its own art: `RoomBase.surface_colors()` samples the sprites on its wall bodies, leaving out the floor's colour. Set `debris_colors` (light, base, shadow) on its `.tres` only when the sample looks wrong. Which motes they are is `surface_kinds` (mote kind ids, content/motes): empty, every `surface` kind (the crypt's chips); a room of another material names its own.
- Its `Floor` sprite is at `z_index` `RoomBase.FLOOR_Z` (-2), so Feel's floor marks (one above it) lie on the floor and under every body (0); `tests/gameplay/feel/test_marks.gd` checks every room. The marks drawn in the floor's own shades take its colours from its art (`RoomBase.floor_colors()`: the `Floor` sprite's most common colours times its modulate); set `floor_mark_colors` (light, base, shadow) only when the sample looks wrong.

## Allowed files

`.tscn` (text), `.tres`, plus `.uid` and `.license` files. Limit: 1 MB per file. Rooms hold layout only and carry no game scripts (their placements and zones, editor scripts, aside).

## Steps

1. Copy the example room into the right biome folder and rename it.
2. Paint the layout with the shared tileset; place doors and spawn points.
3. Edit the `.tres` values. Commit the `.uid` files Godot creates.
4. Run the checks below, then open a PR with a screenshot of the room.

## Example to copy

`content/rooms/crypt/arena_01.tscn` and `arena_01.tres`: a 20×12-tile (640×384 px) crypt arena with a wall border, six pillars for cover, four player spawns in a row at the bottom, six enemy spawns, an entry (bottom) and exit (top) door, and two exits (`room_exit` props) below the top door. Pillars look like 2.5D columns: the collision stays in `Walls` at the base footprint (one tile), a base sprite sits on it (`Pillars`, drawn under characters), and a top sprite one tile higher (`PillarTops`, `z_index` 5) is drawn over characters, so players and enemies walk behind the upper part; the floor shadow is a polygon in `Shade`, under the bases. Torches are an instance of `content/rooms/crypt/decals/torch.tscn`: a bracket, a looping flame (an `AnimatedSprite2D`) and a flickering additive glow, each from frames shared by every torch; the room sets only each instance's `speed_scale` and start `frame`, so the flames are out of step. No script runs any of it. Each torch is also a light for spell light: a `Marker2D` named `Light` at the flame, in the group `light_spot`, with `metadata/light_art` naming the flame, so it lights the room round it in its flame's colour (core/base/light_spot.gd has the other metadata). Its painted glow (the additive `Glow` and `Core` sprites) is in the group `painted_light`, so spell light hides it while it draws the spot's light and shows it again when the setting is off: a torch is lit once (S6, item 12). A room's `ambient` in its `.tres` darkens it where nothing lights it (1.0, the default, is the room as drawn; the sandbox's is 0.85). The scene is layout only: the floor and walls are `Sprite2D` nodes with the shared tiles from `art/tilesets/crypt/` repeated, the walls are one `StaticBody2D` on physics layer 1 (the wall layer, see the player README), and spawns and doors are `Marker2D` nodes under `PlayerSpawns`, `EnemySpawns` and `Doors`, the only place their positions are written (`tests/content/rooms/test_arena_01_data.gd` checks its data and that the scene has no scripts).

The crypt's other fight rooms (run generation RG3b), made like arena_01 from the same tiles, so a run's map has templates to choose from (`kinds: fight`, `biome: crypt`), each with four player spawns along the bottom, eight enemy spawns, an entry door at the bottom and an exit door at the top, and two exits (`room_exit` props, as the arena's) below it:

- `arena_02` ("Crypt nave", 24×12 tiles, 768×384 px): a long hall, two colonnades of four pillars along it and a sarcophagus (a low wall block, `Walls/Block1`) in the middle of the nave.
- `arena_03` ("Ossuary court", 20×14 tiles, 640×448 px): two low walls above a court and two below it, a gap in the middle of each row and the sides open, two pillars in the court and one by each side wall (32 px from it: the narrowest way in the crypt's rooms).
- `arena_04` ("Crypt crossing", 22×14 tiles, 704×448 px): four sarcophagi in the corners and a ring of four pillars round the crossing in the middle.

A low wall is a `CollisionShape2D` in `Walls` drawn by a `wall_top` sprite of its own size (a raised block, as the border's top), with its shadow in `Shade`; a pillar is drawn as arena_01's. **Every template is checked by itself** (found by folder, `RoomScan.templates`): `test_template_layout.gd` (no scripts, the spawn and door groups only markers, each exit open floor clear of every spawn, an exit for each way a run's room may lead on, spawns inside the room, clear of walls and of each other, doors on the border, every spawn and every floor cell reached from the first player spawn), `test_template_art.gd` (the floor covers the room, every wall drawn and every wall sprite on a wall, decoration never blocks and a rubble or skull never lies over a wall), `test_template_pillars.gd` (a pillar's base, top and shadow) and `test_template_torches.gd` (looping, out of step, shared frames); `tests/screenshots/shot_rooms.gd` shows each as the game plays it.

The boss's arena (boss plan OB6, docs/milestones/boss-old-broom.md), `content/rooms/crypt/boss_broom/` (`boss_broom.tscn` and `.tres`, id `boss_broom`, "The broom's crypt"): the Old Broom's best room, `biome: crypt`, `kinds: boss`, so the crypt run's last row stands in it and its boss comes from the run's boss pool (any boss of the crypt; rooms hold layout only, decision 17). 26×16 tiles (832×512 px, about 1.7 times arena_01), the same tiles, walls all round and an open middle, an entry door at the bottom and no exit door and no `room_exit`: cleared, the run is won. Its cover is props, not walls (`PropStandIn`s under `Props`): four **cracked pillars** (`content/props/cracked_pillar`) in a loose diamond round the middle, each broken by the fight (cracked, then rubble), and four **dust heaps** (`content/props/dust_heap`) near the corners, to scatter by walking and to blow up with fire. Its first enemy spawn, top middle, is where the boss stands (Flow stands the room's enemies at its spawns in turn); the four in the corners are for the adds its phases call later (OB13). Ten torches round the walls. It is the broom's (its deletion, decision 28): deleting the boss deletes this folder and this paragraph; no code or test names it. Besides the template tests above, `test_template_layout.gd` checks for every template that its spawns are clear of its props and, for a room that hosts a boss, that every boss of its biome fits at its first enemy spawn, clear of the walls and the props.

The sandbox room (staffs B7), `content/rooms/crypt/sandbox.tscn` and `sandbox.tres` (id `sandbox`): two shooting lanes split by a wall down the middle that stops three tiles short of the bottom, so players walk between the lanes; two player spawns at the bottom of each lane; its `EnemyStandIn`s put the training dummy at the top of the left lane and the shooting dummy at the top of the right one, then a group of four more training dummies round the first in the left lane, to try homing, pierce and spreads on. Test: `tests/content/rooms/test_sandbox_data.gd`.

The hub (docs/milestones/online-lobby.md, L1), `content/rooms/hub/hub.tscn` and `hub.tres` (id `hub`): the room the main menu's Play goes to, where players join and start a run. A large island (40×26 tiles) floating in the void, very dark, its edge drawn by motes (the owner's pick "A, mixed elements", 2026-11-10): `Void`, the void's tile over the whole room at z -3; `Floor` and `Floor2` to `Floor11`, the island's dark slab floor as row bands at `RoomBase.FLOOR_Z`; `Walls`, a collision rectangle over every void tile (the island's edge is a wall); `Rim`, a hidden `Line2D` in the group `mote_source` round the island's edge, clockwise, with its look as an inline `MoteSource` line (core/base/mote_source.gd: `rim_ember` motes, 172.8 a second over the whole rim, each cooling through one of fire's, spark's, arc's, ice's or void's palette shades), which Feel gives off (`gameplay/feel/source_motes.gd`; content/motes/README.md): **the look is that node's data and the mote kind (`content/motes/rim_ember.tres`), so changing it touches no code, and deleting the node leaves a dark island with no rim.** The teleporter stone in the middle (96×96, stood on where its own area is) and the multiplayer altar on the right (a dais; guests appear beside it) are **props** (props W8: `content/props/hub_stone`, `hub_altar`), stood by their `PropStandIn`s under `Props` (each at the middle of its slab), self-contained: the hub's rules (`HubRules`) know them by their role (`run_start`, `party_altar`) and read where they are stood on from their own area; the hub's rules (`HubRules`) ask the stone prop to enter `lit` while every wizard stands on it and `unlit` otherwise, so its glowing runes, glimmers, light and cue are the prop's look. The **prop yard** (the `RoomZone` `prop_yard`, above the stone) is where every prop the registry knows with no role stands (`PropYard`), in rows by id, each fresh again 10 seconds (`PropYard.RETURN_SECONDS`) after it is lit, broken or gone: a new prop's folder is all it takes to try it there. The **rack** (items I6): the Wild star, a premade staff (`content/items/staffs/wild_star`), lying left of the spawns (an `ItemStandIn` under `Items`), and below it a row of five premade builds on the sandbox staff (`Items/GlacierEngine`, `HammerOfTheFaithless`, `CorpseBloom`, `DeathFromEverySky`, `TheMutiny`, 64 px apart so only one is in reach at a time), each taken with A like any item and there again each time the hub starts. Three training dummies together on the left (`EnemyStandIn`s under `Dummies`), four player spawns below the stone. No enemy spawns and no kind hosted: no fight there. `invincible = true`: hits land and take nothing. The art is `art/tilesets/hub/` (void, floor) and the props' folders (stone, lit stone, altar). The room's size is its walls' extent (`RoomPlaces.size`). The scene is written from one island shape by a small generator (not in the repository: the shape is the row spans of the floor; the walls and the rim's points follow from it), so keep the floor bands, the wall rectangles and the rim in step by hand if you edit it; `tests/content/rooms/test_hub_data.gd` checks that every 8-pixel cell is floor or wall and never both, that nothing starts in a wall or on the stone, that the stone prop stands on the island, clear of the spawns, that the yard is open floor clear of the stone, the altar and every spawn, and that the rim runs clockwise with the void on its left all the way round. Its multiplayer altar (L2) is where the altar prop's area is, which its art covers: a wizard standing in it may open the party to other machines with A. **The lectern's place** (run generation RG7): the `RoomZone` `start_lectern`, right of the stone, where the game stands the registry's lectern (`content/props/start_lectern`, by its role); a wizard at it picks with A the depth the stone's run starts at. Deleting the lectern's folder leaves the zone empty and the hub whole; `test_hub_data.gd` checks the lectern there and where it is stood at are open floor clear of the stone, the altar, the yard and every spawn.


## Review questions

Besides the general review:

- Is there space for 4 players to move and dodge?
- Are doors and spawns valid and reachable?
- Is it interesting to fight in (cover, hazards, flow) without hiding enemies from view?
