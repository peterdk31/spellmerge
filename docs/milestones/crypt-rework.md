## What is rough today

Seen in `tests/screenshots/shot_rooms.gd`'s shots on main (2026-11-16, `room_arena_01` to `room_boss_broom`), next to `shot_hub.gd`'s:

1. **Every line is straight.** The walls are bands of the same 32 px brick tile on all four sides, the floor a grid of the same brick tile, the low walls and sarcophagi boxes of it. This is what the owner said of the old hub ("way too 'square' and straight"), and the new hub has no straight line left.
2. **The floor is louder than the fight.** A saturated purple brick, with a mortar line every 16 px across the whole room, sits behind every shot, enemy and wizard. The moss fills large patches of it, so the floor is the busiest thing on screen.
3. **The rooms only differ in where the pillars stand.** The antechamber, the nave, the ossuary court, the crossing and the broom's hall have one floor, one wall and one light, so the run's rooms read as the same room. Nothing in a room is a place you remember: no centrepiece, nothing that says what the room was for.
4. **The light is flat.** Torches have a small glow, and the room is evenly lit right up to the walls. Nothing is dark, so nothing feels dangerous, and nothing is warm, so the torches don't read as safety either.
5. **It no longer matches the hub.** The Landing's ground is drawn per pixel, with motes along its coast and cracks into the void, and its light from above and soft light are coming (HR4, HR5). Coming down from it into a tiled room is a drop in quality, not just a change of place.

## The idea

**The Vaults: the crypt under the Landing.** The hub rework (question 1, answered) puts the crypt directly under the Landing. The Vaults are the old burial halls down there: dug by someone long before the wizard, cracked by the weight of the island hanging above, and pierced by its roots. Today's rooms keep their names and layouts, but each one is drawn as a hall carved from a ragged outline rather than a box:

- **Organic halls.** Every wall follows a rough curve. Masonry gives way to rubble where it has fallen, and the floor's edge is a lip of broken stones and grit. The wall that faces the camera has a real masonry face, so the room reads as dug down into the dark.
- **Quiet flagstones.** The floor is large, irregular flagstones of one quiet shade, softly jointed, dusty toward the walls and in the corners. It sits behind the fight instead of competing with it.
- **Warm against cold.** Torches and candles cast warm pools of light, and the corners fall away into the dark. **A shaft of pale light falls from a crack in the vault** in most rooms: the Landing's own light from above ("Home is up"), with motes of dust drifting in it. It is the hub's beam (HR4), seen from below.
- **One landmark a room,** each with its own light: the antechamber's broken column under the shaft, the nave's sarcophagus ringed by candles, the ossuary's walls of skulls, the crossing's fallen-in floor and the broom's swept ring.
- **The floor gives way.** In two rooms the floor has fallen in and left **pits**: holes (the hub's, docs/milestones/hub-rework.md, "Holes"). Wizards walk round them and dash over them, and an enemy knocked in is gone. A faint cold light comes up from the deep below (named no further: what lies below the crypt is open, docs/STORY.md), dust falls in, and cracks run out from the edge. These are the hub's cracks again, in the crypt's own material.

**Mockups** (drawn at 1×, with stand-ins for the wizards, enemies and props; made in the session that wrote this, not part of the repository; attached to the plan's PR for the owner and the builder):

The mockups are darker than the game would be (question 3): they set the mood, and CR3's screenshots in a real fight set the levels (rule 6).

## The rules

1. **Look only, except for pits.** Every room keeps its rules, placements, events and state. Each new piece is drawn: the ground's art, decals, `light_spot`s, `mote_source`s and Feel's view. A pit is a hole, so it uses the engine HR-H1 and HR-H2 built (walking stops at its edge, dashes, teleports and pushes cross it, a body that ends over it falls, the AI walks round it and shoots across it); nothing new in `core/` or `gameplay/`. Determinism, replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged; `test_snapshot_game.gd` plays a wave in every room, as today. **The AI has no pathfinder** (it steers with `AISight.detour` and `walk_clear`, gameplay/ai/README.md), and a pit inside a ring of pillars is the case where steering could pin an enemy. So a new test plays every enemy spawn of a room with a pit against a wizard at each player spawn, and fails if an enemy does not come within its reach in ten seconds.
2. **The scene is the one place a position is written** (content/rooms/README.md). The floor, the walls' art, their faces and the pits are drawn from a shape file (`<room>.shape.json`) by `tools/ground_shape.py`, as the hub's are. Pillars, low walls, doors, torches, candles, decor, spawns and props stay in the room's scene, edited by hand. **The walls stay rectangles:** the generator covers every wall pixel's cell with 8 px boxes, as it does for any `"void": "wall"` shape, so the walls follow the drawn outline within half a cell. Today a room's border walls are 32 px boxes. Drawn, they wander **in only by a set amount** (the shape's `wander`, 0 to 16 px past the 32 px line, never more) and **out** into bays and alcoves; three things hold them (generator work, "Built for every room"):
   - **clear of what stands in the room:** an outline is pushed back from every keep-clear circle round a placement, spawn, exit, enemy spawn and decor sprite (today the generator keeps only its cracks clear: arena_01's `Enemy2` at (224, 64), its rubble at (40, 48), the exits at y 64 and arena_04's `Enemy2` at (64, 64) sit within 32 px of the border);
   - **pinned where something stands on the wall:** a shape's `pins` hold the outline at exactly the 32 px line along a stretch: under each door (a door's sprite needs the wall's full depth), under each torch, and along arena_03's side ways, whose 32 px beside `Pillar1` and `Pillar2` is the narrowest way in the crypt and stays so;
   - **the straight shade polygons go:** `Shade/TopShade` and `LeftShade` drew a wall's shadow on a straight edge; the generator's face (with its dark foot) takes their place. The pillars' and low walls' shades stay.
3. **Organic lines everywhere** (the owner's rule for the hub, 2026-11-16). No straight outline: the halls' edges, the pits, the dust drifts, the sarcophagi's broken corners all follow curves with noise in them. The 32 grid stays where things stand and are sized. **Low walls and sarcophagi** stay boxes in `Walls`, each drawn by a sprite of its box's own size (`test_every_wall_sprite_draws_over_a_collision_shape`): the art fills its box, its outer silhouette organic only inside the box (chipped corners drawn in, never past), so the collision and the picture never disagree.
4. **The pillars stay 2.5D** (content/rooms/README.md, "Example to copy"): a base on its one-tile wall box, a top drawn over bodies, a shadow, all checked by `test_template_pillars.gd`. They are drawn again as round columns with chipped capitals. The broken column keeps a top sprite (a jagged stump, 32×32, translucent where it is broken), so the test holds for it unchanged. They stay shared art in `art/tilesets/crypt/`.
5. **Static decoration is the room's own decals,** as the torches are today (`content/rooms/crypt/decals/`): candles (a cluster, a looping flame, a `light_spot` and a `painted_light` glow, like a torch), the light shaft (HR4's beam decal, shared, `content/rooms/decor/`), hanging roots, bone piles, the broken column's drums, the sarcophagus's lid with its figure. No script runs any of it, and no decal blocks or lies over a wall (`test_template_art.gd`, which learns to check instanced decals, "Tests that change").
6. **Readable first, and measured.** Shots, enemies and wizards are the brightest and most saturated things on screen, as docs/STYLE.md's "Readability" asks. The floor is one quiet value of Stone base, its joints and details in Stone shadow (the guide's rule for floors). The dark is in the walls' art and toward them, never where the fight is:
   - **a number:** in the middle two thirds of a room, the ground art's floor pixels average at least the luminance of Stone base, and no 32 px cell of it averages below Stone shadow's (`test_template_art.gd`, read from the art, so it holds with spell light off);
   - **the room's `ambient`:** 0.8 for a crypt room (the sandbox has 0.85), set in its `.tres`, darkening it evenly where no light reaches when spell light is on;
   - **with spell light off,** no `ambient` is applied, so the crypt keeps its mood from its art alone: the walls' art and the face fall into the dark, the floor does not;
   - **the shaft** is a `light_spot` (it lights the floor in its colour while spell light is on) with its painted column in `painted_light`, as a torch is: lit once either way;
   - **a shot of a real fight:** `shot_rooms.gd` gains a shot a room a few seconds into its fight, with spell light on and off (`room_<id>_fight`, `room_<id>_fight_plain`). The session checks the antechamber's before it goes on.
7. **Past the style guide where it helps, as the hub went** (question 4, which lists each place it does). The ground's art is a PNG drawn per pixel, as the hub's is. The 32 grid, the pixel look and the outline colour stay. The crypt's colours stay in the palette's Stone, Bone, Wood and Fire, plus Mana for the pits' cold light, so the crypt still looks like the crypt next to its enemies. The pits' cold light changes only by frames of whole-pixel shapes (the "Smooth" rule), never by an alpha pulse; the plan's pick is a still glow and the falling dust as the only movement.
8. **The cobwebs stay, the moss goes** (question 5: "Much less. Not even sure it is needed at all"): the crypt's rooms grow no moss (`crypt_moss` no longer names the crypt among its `biomes`, and the rooms' moss fields go); the cobwebs stay in the corners, at most 6% of a room's floor as today. If nothing else grows `crypt_moss`, its folder goes in CR3. A tuft of roots' moss, if a room wants one, is decor.
9. **A room drawn from a shape names its floor-mark and debris colours** in its `.tres`, as the hub does, so its large ground art is never sampled.
10. **Every reworked test passes for tiled rooms and shaped ones alike,** since CR3 to CR6 leave the crypt mixed.

## Built for every room

As with the hub, nothing here is the crypt's alone except its shape files and where its things stand. Every piece is general, documented in its area's README, and tested on fixture shapes (`tools/test_ground_shape.py`), not only through the crypt. The generator's `VERSION` goes up with it.

|---|---|---|
| **Outlines held clear and pinned** | An outline is pushed back from every keep-clear circle (as cracks already are), may wander in no more than its `wander`, and is held to a straight line along each of its `pins` (a stretch of the room's edge, `[from, to]` on one side, at a given depth) | `tools/ground_shape.py` (`Ground`) |
| **Wall art** | Where the void is wall, the wall's own picture: its top (an edge style's `top`: rough stone, rubble where noise says, falling into the dark away from the floor) and its face, drawn into an image of its own (`<room>_ground_wall.png`), a sprite on the `Walls` body (so a hit sheds its colours, `surface_colors()`), never into the floor's image, which stays opaque only where a body may stand. Today a `"void": "wall"` room draws nothing over its walls but the rock face under its bottom edge | `tools/ground_shape.py`; `edge.json` (`top`) |
| **A wall face** | An edge style's optional `face`: the masonry face drawn on edges with **wall above and floor below** (the wall the camera looks at), on outlines only: its height, courses and shades, a dark line at its foot. Its pixels lie in the wall's cells and its image | `edge.json` (`face`) |
| **Optional parts of an edge style** | `rock`, `seam`, `glow`, `rim` and `drip` become optional in `edge.json` (today all are required and read directly): `crypt_wall` has no seam, glow or motes and hangs no rock face under the floor's bottom edge (that would be a cliff, not a wall) | `tools/ground_shape.py`; content/edges/README.md |
