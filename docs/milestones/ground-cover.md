# Ground cover: grass and other cover that sways, burns and is trampled

**Status (2026-11-15): draft, being iterated with the owner.** Not approved; nothing is built. It builds on the decoration conventions the props plan's room review set (docs/milestones/props.md, "W8c: a room foundation", "Decoration, for later"; content/rooms/README.md, "Decoration") and the named but unbuilt `room_built` event (W8c's second review).

**Owner's answers so far (2026-11-15):** all three tiers (looks, sways as bodies walk through, and changes the game); placed both by hand and by each biome's own fill rule ("we already have a room generator"); low ground cover for now (drawn under every body); in the hub and the crypt.

The steps are **GC1 to GC9** (GC for ground cover). Each is one PR with its tests, in order.

## What the players see

- The hub's island is grassy; the crypt's floors have their own cover (moss, bone dust, cobwebs: question 3) in clumps, never under a wall.
- Grass sways in a slow wind and bends away as a wizard, an enemy or a rolling barrel goes through it, springing back behind them.
- **Fire lights it.** A fire blast, pulse or pool, or a fire shot ending on the floor, sets the grass there burning; it spreads to the grass beside it, burns whatever stands in it (the burn status, as any burn: resisted by the body's stats), lights dust heaps and barrels standing in it (their own triggers: a burn on them), and leaves ash. Frost puts burning cover out. Cobwebs slow whoever walks through them (the chill status) until burnt away.
- Bosses and enemies read burning ground as danger and walk round it; so do the test bots.

## The rules

1. **One layout, worked out from the room, never state.** A room's cover is a grid of cells (16 px, half a tile), each holding a cover kind or none: `CoverLayout.of(room_layout, places, walls, biome_kinds, room_id)` (core/base), a pure function of the built room. Two sources, in order: the biome's **fill** (each `CoverKind` whose `biomes` names the room's biome fills open floor by its own rule: density, clump size, how far it keeps from walls, doors and spawns), then the room's **fields** (`CoverField`, a `RoomZone` with data, `data()` as W8c named it: a kind and a density over its rectangle; a field of no kind clears). A room's `.tres` may turn the fill off (`cover_fill = false`). The clumps come from a hash of the room's id, the kind and the cell (the engine's hash of a `Vector4i`, integer code), not from `sim.chance`, so the editor, the game and every machine work out the same layout and no roll of the run moves. Since it is derived, every area that needs it works it out again (`rebuild_derived` after a snapshot).
2. **One event for a built room.** Flow sends `room_built(room_id, layout_root, places)` once the room shown is built (W8c's named event); the ground area, Feel and the AI's danger map hear it. Nothing calls them.
3. **What it looks like and what it does are one kind's data.** A cover kind is one content folder (`content/cover/<id>/`: its `.tres` on `CoverKind`, the tuft frames, `assets.json`, its test), discovered by folder. Its **states** are rows like a prop's (`PropStates`, the same shape on purpose): a state's look (which tuft frames, sway, bend), what it does to a body standing in it (a status id, strength and how often; none for most), whether it spreads and what (an element to its neighbours, a chance a second), and its triggers to the next state: `element` (fire, frost, ... touched it), `walked` (a body stood in it for so long; trampled), `after` (seconds). The grass: `rest` −fire→ `burning` (1.2 s, burn on standers, spreads fire) −after→ `ash` (−after 30 s→ `rest` in the hub only: question 2); `burning` −frost→ `rest`; `rest` −walked→ `trampled` (question 4). A new kind of cover is new files; a new kind of trigger is engine work.
4. **The game's part is one simulation area, not an actor a tuft** (W8c). `Ground` (gameplay/ground, a new area; simulation, so its members are state unless listed) joins the Tick under a key of `Tick.STEPS` after the bodies (it reads where they stood after their move), before Items. **State:** each cell's state and its seconds in it (packed arrays, all rest at a room's start), the active cells (those not at rest, a packed list, stepped alone), each body's time in its cell (for `walked`) and its next effect tick, keyed by actor id; the counters of its rolls. **Derived:** the layout and the kinds. A room where nothing has happened hashes as a run of zeros. Spread rolls are `sim.chance(&"cover_spread", cell, counter)`. An effect is asked as any status is (`Events.status_requested`), so Combat applies it by its own rules and the ground never calls Combat.
5. **Elements reach the floor by one event.** Spells (core) tells where an element touched the floor: `ground_touched(caster, element, at, radius)` when an elemental blast or pulse goes off, an elemental pool lies (once, then every 0.25 s while it lies) and an elemental shot ends (it hit a body or a wall, or ran out). A shot in flight touches nothing (question 1). The ground area hears it and fires the cells' `element` triggers inside the circle, in cell order, at its next step. Spells knows nothing of cover.
6. **Feel draws it, from the layout and the changes.** `gameplay/feel/cover.gd` (its own script, never more of `feel.gd`) hears `room_built`, scatters each covered cell's tufts (a hash of the cell; the same tufts the editor shows) into one batch per kind (`Batches.multimesh()`), at the z between the floor marks and the bodies. Its shader sways every tuft with a wind (view time) and bends it away from the nearest bodies (a fixed array of at most 32 positions, those nearest the camera, set each frame without allocating); a tuft's state picks its frames through the instance's custom data. The ground area tells what changed (`ground_cover_changed(cells, states)`, a tick's changes in one event); Feel updates those tufts only, and gives a burning cell embers and smoke through the mote sources it already has. The shader is listed in the shader warmup. The look follows the state, never the other way round.
7. **The editor shows what the game shows.** `CoverField` (core/base, an editor script) draws the tufts of its rectangle in the editor from `CoverLayout` and the kind's rest frames, and the room's scene shows the biome's fill the same way (one editor script on the room's `Floor`, `CoverPreview`, tools/), so moving a field or a wall in the editor moves the grass.
8. **The AI reads it through the bus.** The ground area answers where the floor is dangerous (`ground_danger_looked(at, reach, sight)`, as `ground_looked` fills a `GroundSight`); the brain's path and its `keep range` and `evade` steer off cells whose state harms its body. The AI never calls the ground area.

## Cost

A crypt arena is about 1,700 cells; the boss's arena 1,664 at 16 px. Only active cells are stepped; a body's cell is one lookup. Bodies standing in harmful cover are a handful. The hash of a resting room is a few packed arrays of zeros. The look is one batch a kind and one shader; the bend's cost goes with the bodies (at most 32), not the tufts. GC6 measures it: a benchmark (`tests/benchmark/cover_cost.gd`) of the largest room fully covered, all of it burning at once, with 100 bodies in it, a tick's cost and the frame's, against main. No native kernel unless that benchmark asks for one (then it mirrors the GDScript to the bit, as every kernel does).

## Questions (open)

1. **What lights it:** only where fire lands (blasts, pulses, pools, a shot's end), or also the floor under a fire shot in flight? *Recommended: where it lands. A flying shot lighting a line of grass behind it every tick would set most of a room alight in any fight with fire, and costs a check a shot a tick.*
2. **Does burnt cover grow back?** *Recommended: data per kind; the hub's grass in 30 s (the lobby is played for long), the crypt's never within a room (a fight changes its room).*
3. **The crypt's cover:** which of moss (damp: does not burn, frost makes it slick?), bone dust (trampled into paths, look only), cobwebs (slow, burn away fast), pale fungus (glows; bursts into spores on a hit?), or others? *Recommended for the first pass: moss and cobwebs, one that is look only and one that plays.*
4. **Trampled:** look only (a flattened state that springs back after a while), or does it play (trampled grass catches fire more slowly, so a party can walk a firebreak)? *Recommended: look only first; a playing trample is a row of data later.*
5. **When:** the roadmap builds one milestone at a time and M2 is open; this needs the owner's exception, as props, items and the broom had, or it waits for M3/M4. And beside which lane: after run generation (RG5-RG8), or alongside it?
