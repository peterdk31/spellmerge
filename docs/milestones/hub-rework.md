# Hub rework: the Landing

**Status (2026-11-16): a draft for the owner.** Asked by the owner: "Can you come up with a complete rework of the hub? Right now it looks kinda rough. It should look cool and inviting." Nothing here is built. The steps are **HR1 to HR6** (HR for hub rework), one PR each with its tests, in order, once the owner has answered the questions at the end. It changes how the hub looks and where things stand in it, and adds two general rules to the game: a placed prop may start in a state of its own (for the campfire), and **holes** (for the cracks: the section "Holes"). Otherwise it never changes what the hub does: the stone, the altar, the lectern, the rack, the dummies, the prop yard, joining and the run's start all work as today (docs/milestones/online-lobby.md, items.md, run-generation.md, ground-cover.md).

## What is rough today

Seen in `tests/screenshots/shot_hub.gd`'s shots on main (2026-11-16):

1. **The floor is nearly black.** `art/tilesets/hub/floor.svg` is Outline (`#14121f`) with Stone shadow joints, almost the same colour as the void's own tile (`void.svg`), and the room's `ambient` (0.9) darkens it again, so the grass is the only thing that shows.
2. **You cannot see where the island ends.** The void and the floor are almost the same colour, so the edge is only the rim's embers; the "island floating in the void" does not read as an island. Its outline is a staircase of rectangles.
3. **Nothing is a place.** The stone, the lectern, the altar, the dummies and the rack each stand alone on the same dark bricks, with nothing round them saying what they are for or how to get there. The rack is six staffs lying in a row on the floor.
4. **No warmth.** The one light is the stone's, and only once everyone is on it. Nothing in the hub says "stay a while".

## The idea

**The Landing: where the dropped staffs land.** The canon already says a staff that loses a run "lands at the bottom of the shaft again" (docs/STORY.md, canon 7), and the hub's shape is open (STORY.md, "Not fixed yet"). So the hub becomes that spot: the shaft opens into a great dark hollow, and an island of turf and old stone hangs in it, with a column of pale light falling on it from the hole far above ("Home is up"). The staffs who landed here before made it a camp: a fire, a rack of staffs, a practice ring, a yard of the wizard's junk. The crypt's rooms lie below it, and the teleporter stone takes you down into them. **This moves the story:** today the intro (`gameplay/flow/intro.gd`) and canon 4 put the bottom of the shaft on the crypt's floor. With a yes to question 1, the intro's last shot lands on the Landing instead (its floor redrawn; HR5) and canon 4 says the shaft ends on the Landing, with the crypt under it.

The owner's picks stay: an island floating in the void, the rim of embers in the elements' colours (L1, "A, mixed elements"), the stone in the middle, the altar, the dummies together. The void stays dark, because a dark frame is what makes a warm, lit island look inviting.

## The rules

1. **The look only, but for the campfire and the holes.** The hub keeps its rules, its props, its events and its state. Every new piece is drawn: sprites, decals, `light_spot`s, `mote_source`s and Feel's view. The one new body is the fire pit (an existing prop, placed; rule 9). No new simulation member, so determinism, replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged; the content determinism and snapshot tests cover the hub as they do today.
2. **The scene is the one place a position is written** (content/rooms/README.md), so everything moved is moved in `hub.tscn`, and the owner can keep moving it in the editor.
3. **The island's shape is a file, its scene written from it.** Today the scene is "written from one island shape by a small generator (not in the repository)", so its floor bands, walls and rim must be kept in step by hand. The rework adds the shape as data (`content/rooms/hub/island.json`: the coastline as a smooth closed curve, its points and how rough it is, its seed; the islets the same) and the generator to `tools/` (`tools/hub_island.py`, with `tools/test_hub_island.py`), which draws the island's art from it per pixel (the turf, the lip, the underside, as PNGs cut in tiles of at most 1 MB) and works out its walls and rim, and writes them into a scene of their own, `content/rooms/hub/island.tscn` (the floor sprites, the wall rectangles and their shapes, the rim's points, the underside's sprites), instanced in `hub.tscn`. The generator never touches `hub.tscn`, so the owner can save it in the editor freely (the editor rewrites the whole file, so markers in it would not survive); everything placed by hand stays there. A test fails when `island.tscn` and the shape disagree. The underside's sprites lie over the void (wall), so they are not named `Floor*`, and `test_hub_data.gd`, which today reads only the room's direct `Floor*` children, reads them through the instance.
4. **Organic lines everywhere** (the owner, 2026-11-16). No straight edge and no tile-shaped edge anywhere the eye goes: the coastline, the clearings of earth, the slabs' broken rims, the paths and the fence all follow curves with noise in them, and the grass grows over the edges between them. The 32 grid stays where things stand and are sized, never in an outline. **The walls stay rectangles:** the game's walls are boxes only (the cover reads them through `RoomPlaces.wall_rects`, the bodies' and shots' walls through `Flow._set_walls`, and both take only `RectangleShape2D`), so the generator covers the void with 8 px wall boxes merged into runs, a staircase of 8 px steps hidden under the drawn edge, the coast's art reaching a few pixels past the walls so no step shows. This costs nothing a frame: the projectile manager already lays every wall into one grid of 8 px cells as a room starts, and the bodies slide against that grid, so a wizard already walks on 8 px steps today. The grass's 16 px cells stop where a wall touches them, so its edge steps in 16 px a little inside the drawn coast; HR2's screenshots check that it does not show. The rim's points follow the drawn coastline, not the steps.
5. **Static decoration is the room's own decals,** as the crypt's torches are (`content/rooms/crypt/decals/torch.tscn`): lanterns, the campfire's logs, the rack's frame, the fence, the islets, the beam, in `content/rooms/hub/decals/`, scenes of sprites (and an `AnimationPlayer` where something moves) with no script, each lantern a `light_spot` with its painted glow in `painted_light`. Decor is never a prop: every prop with no role would stand in the junk yard (`PropYard`).
6. **docs/STYLE.md is a starting point here, not a rule** (the owner, 2026-11-16: "The style guide is not necessarily to be followed"). The hub keeps the 32 grid and the pixel look, so it sits beside the crypt and the players, but it may go past the guide where that makes it look better: soft light and glow (the beam from above, the lanterns' and the fire's halos), colours and shades outside the palette (a warmer turf, a deep blue for the void), gradients in the void and the beam, and art drawn as PNGs instead of rect-only SVGs. Each step's screenshots are the check, not the style checklist. The hard rules (file types, sizes, licences, the manifest) still hold, and so does the code's own art test where it checks every SVG's colours (`tests/core/test_svg_art.gd`): art past the palette is a PNG, or the test learns which files may be. docs/STYLE.md gets a line saying the hub's art may go past it. The room's `ambient` goes back to 1.0.
7. **The grass's floor.** The cover's tufts (`content/cover/hub_grass/tufts.svg`) are Leaf shadow and base, so on a green floor their shadow pixels vanish (found drawing the mockups). With turf, the tufts are drawn again a shade lighter (Leaf base and light, or a shade of the hub's own) over a turf darker than them: see question 2.
8. **The seats' crystals use the player colours:** a crystal is a seat's marker, as a HUD panel's border is, so a player colour on screen still means a player. A crystal is lit while its seat is taken, from any machine: Feel hears `seat_joined` and `seat_left` (core/events), which every machine hears for every seat.
9. **A placed prop may start in a state of its own** (for the campfire; the one change to the simulation). `PropStandIn` gains `starts_in` (a state id of its prop; empty, its first, as today), apart from its `state`, which stays the editor's view only (no room sets one today; its doc's "the game always starts it in its first" changes). The game stands the prop in that state, not entered: no cue, no `entered` triggers; its hurt shape, footprint, look, light and motes are built from the state the body holds when it is made (`PropStateTriggers.setup`, `PropLook.setup`), so they are that state's. The state is the prop's own, hashed, so replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged. The chain, data only: `RoomPlacement.starts_in()` (empty by default), overridden by `PropStandIn` (its `prop` setter clears it; the editor shows it when `state` is empty); `RoomPlaces` keeps it beside each placement and names a state its prop lacks in `problems()`; `RoomContents` hands it to `PropBody.make(data, starts_in)`, which sets the body's state before it is ready, and `stand_again` passes it on. Tests: `test_room_places.gd` (read, and the problem), `tests/gameplay/actor/test_prop_look.gd` (a body made lit shows the lit look and light, with no cue), a new flow test (the placed fire pit starts lit, frost puts it out, fire lights it again). **The junk yard still stands its own fire pit,** unlit as today: the fire pit has no role, so the prop yard shows it, and the campfire is a second one.

## Holes

Holes are new to the game and general: the crypt's rooms may have them later (a pit, a broken floor). They are engine work in `core/` and `gameplay/`, built in their own steps (HR-H1 to HR-H3, below), and the hub's cracks are the first. Today a cell is either a wall (room walls, solid shots: walls to everything) or the ground of a body that cannot move (a prop's footprint: a wall to walking bodies only; shots pass); a hole is a third kind.

1. **What a hole is.** A cell a walking body cannot step into, which nothing else minds: shots, beams, rays, sight and a portal's gates pass over it, as over a prop's ground. A room marks its holes as it marks its walls, by rectangles in the scene: a `StaticBody2D` on a hole layer (`RoomBase.HOLE_LAYER`, beside `WALL_LAYER`) with `RectangleShape2D`s (the hub's generator writes 8 px runs over every crack's cells into `island.tscn`). `RoomPlaces.hole_rects` reads them as `wall_rects` reads walls, and Flow hands them to the projectile manager with the walls (`_set_walls`) into a third grid over the same 8 px cells (`set_holes`; DERIVED, laid again as the room is, as the wall grid is).
2. **Walking stops at the lip.** `WallSlide.slide` takes the hole grid and a flag: a body's move slides along holes as along walls unless the move crosses holes. Its own walking (the intent) never does. **Every other move does** (owner: "dashed or teleported over, and portaled to, and probably by other means as well"): a dash (the Mover knows its moves), a teleport (it jumps, and its back-off from walls ignores holes), a portal's exit (`target_move_requested`), and (question 8) knockback, a pull, recoil, a swap and Plow's carry. The native `BodyKernel` mirrors the change line by line (native/README.md), and `tests/core/test_wall_slide.gd` compares the two to the bit with holes in the grid.
3. **A body that ends over a hole falls.** At the end of its step, a body that walks (a Mover) whose centre lies on a hole cell and that has no move crossing holes still running falls: it stops, its moves end (answered, so a dash's `on expire` still fires where it ended), it cannot be hit and casts nothing, and Feel shows it fall (it shrinks and sinks into the void over `HoleRules.FALL_SECONDS`, 0.6, with motes off it; drawing only). Then, by question 10: a wizard comes back on the last ground cell it stood on (kept each step, state; a cell never next to a hole, so it does not fall again at once), protected for a moment as a joiner is; in the hub at no cost (the hub's "a fall costs nothing", 2026-11-13). Its state: the fall's seconds left and the last ground cell, per body, hashed, so replays, snapshots and joining hold. Sent on the bus: `body_fell(actor)` and `body_returned(actor, at)`. A body that cannot move (a prop) stands where it was placed; a prop's ground is never laid over a hole (`RoomPlaces.problems()`).
4. **Who sees holes, besides walking.** The AI walks round them as round props' ground (`ground_clear`, `clear_run`: holes added), and never dashes into one (its moves' end points checked); the bots likewise. The ground cover covers no hole cell (`CoverLayout.of`). Feel's floor marks are not laid over holes. An item dropped over a hole lands at the nearest ground (`Items`). Spawns and placements are never over a hole (`RoomPlaces.problems()`, `test_template_layout.gd`).

## Questions for the owner

1. **The Landing:** the bottom of the shaft, the light from the tower above, a camp the dropped staffs made, with the crypt below it (canon 4 and the intro's last shot change to match)? Or keep it an island in the void with no story place, and the story as it is?
2. **The floor:** turf (Leaf shadow, a dark meadow, the grass over it) with stone only on the plaza, the paths and the slabs? Or stone everywhere with grass on it, as today but lighter?
3. **Brightness:** ambient 1.0 and warm lights everywhere (the mockups), with the void kept dark? Or keep it moody, lit only round the places?
4. **Size:** the mockups' island is about 44×28 tiles (today 40×26), so the places have room between them. Keep that, or fit it into today's size?
5. **The seats' crystals in the player colours** on the altar: yes, or a plain light?
6. **How far past the style guide:** the soft-light mockups (glow, a beam, a deep blue void), or the banded light of the first mockups?
7. **The plaza's runes lit with the stone:** worth making the plaza part of the stone prop (its look grows past its area, and the tests that hold a prop's layers to its area learn an exception)? Or leave the plaza plain room art, with only the stone itself lighting up, as today?
8. **Pushed into a hole:** knockback, pulls, a carry (the broom's sweep) and the like cross holes too, so a hit can knock a wizard in (the plan's pick: it makes the cracks matter, and in the hub a fall costs nothing)? Or does only a move the body chose (a dash, a teleport, a portal) cross, and everything else stop at the lip as walking does?
9. **The island's own edge:** a hole too, so a wizard can dash off the island and fall (the same rule everywhere; in the hub it costs nothing), or a wall as today? The plan's pick: a hole, the void being the same void as in the cracks.
10. **What a fall costs outside the hub:** the plan's pick is a share of the body's most health (20%, a number of `HoleRules`), a wizard back on the ground it last stood on; an enemy or a prop that falls is gone (dead, so a run's room can be cleared by sweeping strays into a crack); a boss never falls (it stops at the lip, as walking does). Or another rule?
