# Hub rework: the Landing

**Status (2026-11-16): approved by the owner** ("Go with your picks!", answering every question at the end with the plan's pick; the answers are written there). HR1 done (this document); **HR2 built** (the island, below, "Built (HR2)"); HR-H1 next. Asked by the owner: "Can you come up with a complete rework of the hub? Right now it looks kinda rough. It should look cool and inviting." The steps are **HR1 to HR6** and **HR-H1 and HR-H2** (HR for hub rework, H for holes), one PR each with its tests, in the order of "Steps". It changes how the hub looks and where things stand in it, and adds two general rules to the game: a placed prop may start in a state of its own (for the campfire), and **holes** (for the cracks: the section "Holes"). Otherwise it never changes what the hub does: the stone, the altar, the lectern, the rack, the dummies, the prop yard, joining and the run's start all work as today (docs/milestones/online-lobby.md, items.md, run-generation.md, ground-cover.md).

## What is rough today

Seen in `tests/screenshots/shot_hub.gd`'s shots on main (2026-11-16):

1. **The floor is nearly black.** `art/tilesets/hub/floor.svg` is Outline (`#14121f`) with Stone shadow joints, almost the same colour as the void's own tile (`void.svg`), and the room's `ambient` (0.9) darkens it again, so the grass is the only thing that shows.
2. **You cannot see where the island ends.** The void and the floor are almost the same colour, so the edge is only the rim's embers; the "island floating in the void" does not read as an island. Its outline is a staircase of rectangles.
3. **Nothing is a place.** The stone, the lectern, the altar, the dummies and the rack each stand alone on the same dark bricks, with nothing round them saying what they are for or how to get there. The rack is six staffs lying in a row on the floor.
4. **No warmth.** The one light is the stone's, and only once everyone is on it. Nothing in the hub says "stay a while".

## The idea

**The Landing: where the dropped staffs land.** The canon already says a staff that loses a run "lands at the bottom of the shaft again" (docs/STORY.md, canon 7), and the hub's shape is open (STORY.md, "Not fixed yet"). So the hub becomes that spot: the shaft opens into a great dark hollow, and an island of turf and old stone hangs in it, with a column of pale light falling on it from the hole far above ("Home is up"). The staffs who landed here before made it a camp: a fire, a rack of staffs, a practice ring, a yard of the wizard's junk. The crypt's rooms lie below it, and the teleporter stone takes you down into them. **This moves the story:** today the intro (`gameplay/flow/intro.gd`) and canon 4 put the bottom of the shaft on the crypt's floor. With a yes to question 1, the intro's last shot lands on the Landing instead (its floor redrawn; HR5) and canon 4 says the shaft ends on the Landing, with the crypt under it.

The owner's picks stay: an island floating in the void, the rim of embers in the elements' colours (L1, "A, mixed elements"), the stone in the middle, the altar, the dummies together. The void stays dark, because a dark frame is what makes a warm, lit island look inviting.

## The rules

1. **The look only, but for the campfire and the holes.** The hub keeps its rules, its props, its events and its state. Every new piece is drawn: sprites, decals, `light_spot`s, `mote_source`s and Feel's view. The one new body is the fire pit (an existing prop, placed; rule 9). No new simulation member, so determinism, replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged; the content determinism and snapshot tests cover the hub as they do today.
2. **The scene is the one place a position is written** (content/rooms/README.md), so everything moved is moved in `hub.tscn`, and the owner can keep moving it in the editor.
3. **The island's shape is a file, its scene written from it** (the general tool of "Built for every room"; the hub is its first room). Today the scene is "written from one island shape by a small generator (not in the repository)", so its floor bands, walls and rim must be kept in step by hand. The rework adds the shape as data (`content/rooms/hub/hub.shape.json`: the coastline as a smooth closed curve, its points and how rough it is, its seed; the islets the same) and the generator to `tools/` (`tools/ground_shape.py`, with `tools/test_ground_shape.py`), which draws the island's art from it per pixel (the turf, the lip, the underside, the glow: one PNG a layer, which the generator refuses to write past 1 MB) and works out its walls and rim, and writes them into a scene of their own, `content/rooms/hub/hub_ground.tscn` (the floor sprite, the wall rectangles and their shapes, the rim's and drips' points, the rock face's sprite, the glow), instanced in `hub.tscn`. The generator never touches `hub.tscn`, so the owner can save it in the editor freely (the editor rewrites the whole file, so markers in it would not survive); everything placed by hand stays there. A test fails when `hub_ground.tscn` and the shape disagree. The underside's sprites lie over the void (wall), so they are not named `Floor*`, and `test_hub_data.gd`, which today reads only the room's direct `Floor*` children, reads them through the instance.
4. **Organic lines everywhere** (the owner, 2026-11-16). No straight edge and no tile-shaped edge anywhere the eye goes: the coastline, the clearings of earth, the slabs' broken rims, the paths and the fence all follow curves with noise in them, and the grass grows over the edges between them. The 32 grid stays where things stand and are sized, never in an outline. **The walls stay rectangles:** the game's walls are boxes only (the cover reads them through `RoomPlaces.wall_rects`, the bodies' and shots' walls through `Flow._set_walls`, and both take only `RectangleShape2D`), so the generator covers the void with 8 px wall boxes merged into runs, a staircase of 8 px steps hidden under the drawn edge, the coast's art reaching a few pixels past the walls so no step shows. This costs nothing a frame: the projectile manager already lays every wall into one grid of 8 px cells as a room starts, and the bodies slide against that grid, so a wizard already walks on 8 px steps today. The grass's 16 px cells stop where a wall touches them, so its edge steps in 16 px a little inside the drawn coast; HR2's screenshots check that it does not show. The rim's points follow the drawn coastline, not the steps.
5. **Static decoration is the room's own decals,** as the crypt's torches are (`content/rooms/crypt/decals/torch.tscn`): lanterns, the campfire's logs, the rack's frame, the fence, the islets, the beam, in `content/rooms/decor/`, scenes of sprites (and an `AnimationPlayer` where something moves) with no script, each lantern a `light_spot` with its painted glow in `painted_light`. Decor is never a prop: every prop with no role would stand in the junk yard (`PropYard`).
6. **docs/STYLE.md is a starting point here, not a rule** (the owner, 2026-11-16: "The style guide is not necessarily to be followed"). The hub keeps the 32 grid and the pixel look, so it sits beside the crypt and the players, but it may go past the guide where that makes it look better: soft light and glow (the beam from above, the lanterns' and the fire's halos), colours and shades outside the palette (a warmer turf, a deep blue for the void), gradients in the void and the beam, and art drawn as PNGs instead of rect-only SVGs. Each step's screenshots are the check, not the style checklist. The hard rules (file types, sizes, licences, the manifest) still hold, and so does the code's own art test where it checks every SVG's colours (`tests/core/test_svg_art.gd`): art past the palette is a PNG, or the test learns which files may be. docs/STYLE.md gets a line saying the hub's art may go past it. The room's `ambient` goes back to 1.0.
7. **The grass's floor.** The cover's tufts (`content/cover/hub_grass/tufts.svg`, Leaf shadow and base) stand over a turf darker than them (`content/grounds/turf`). HR2 tried them a shade lighter, as the mockups drew them; in the game's picture that drowned the turf, so they stay as they were.
8. **The seats' crystals use the player colours:** a crystal is a seat's marker, as a HUD panel's border is, so a player colour on screen still means a player. A crystal is lit while its seat is taken, from any machine: Feel hears `seat_joined` and `seat_left` (core/events), which every machine hears for every seat.
9. **A placed prop may start in a state of its own** (for the campfire; the one change to the simulation). `PropStandIn` gains `starts_in` (a state id of its prop; empty, its first, as today), apart from its `state`, which stays the editor's view only (no room sets one today; its doc's "the game always starts it in its first" changes). The game stands the prop in that state, not entered: no cue, no `entered` triggers; its hurt shape, footprint, look, light and motes are built from the state the body holds when it is made (`PropStateTriggers.setup`, `PropLook.setup`), so they are that state's. The state is the prop's own, hashed, so replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged. The chain, data only: `RoomPlacement.starts_in()` (empty by default), overridden by `PropStandIn` (its `prop` setter clears it; the editor shows it when `state` is empty); `RoomPlaces` keeps it beside each placement and names a state its prop lacks in `problems()`; `RoomContents` hands it to `PropBody.make(data, starts_in)`, which sets the body's state before it is ready, and `stand_again` passes it on. Tests: `test_room_places.gd` (read, and the problem), `tests/gameplay/actor/test_prop_look.gd` (a body made lit shows the lit look and light, with no cue), a new flow test (the placed fire pit starts lit, frost puts it out, fire lights it again). **The junk yard still stands its own fire pit,** unlit as today: the fire pit has no role, so the prop yard shows it, and the campfire is a second one.

Tests: `test_hub_data.gd` (the floor from the art, the walls as a grid, the rim by name with its bands; every source sound), `tests/content/rooms/room_scan.gd` (walls at any depth, through `RoomPlaces.wall_rects`); the screenshot `hub_island` (`shot_hub.gd`). **Choices HR2 made** (the plan left them open):

- **The ground's art is in `art/grounds/<room>/`**, not beside the scene: a room's folder holds its layout (content/rooms/README.md), and every asset is listed in `art/manifest.json`. The scene and the shape file stay with the room.

- **The ground scene's root is the room's `Floor`** (a `Sprite2D` of the floor art, at `FLOOR_Z`), so every reader of a room's `Floor` (its size, its mark colours, the marks' test) reads it unchanged. The glow is a child sprite at half size, added (`CanvasItemMaterial` blend add), at z -1; the rock face is a sprite on the walls' body, so a hit on the edge sheds rock.
- **The hub names its floor-mark and debris colours** in `hub.tres`: sampling 1600×1088 pixels in GDScript would stall the first mark.
- **Everything placed moved by one offset** (160, 160: whole cells of every grid), to the middle of the larger island; HR3 and HR4 place things anew.

- **A drip line runs right to left** (a line throws to the left of its way, so down) and gives off only the colour of the seam band it hangs under; a drip line is split where the band above it changes.
- **A piece of ground that holds no open cell is cleared** (a sliver a crack cuts loose would be drawn over the walls with a rim of its own), and the boundary trace stops by Jacob's rule (a start on a one-pixel neck goes all the way round).
- **The grass keeps its colours** (rule 7 changed): drawn a shade lighter it drowned the turf in the game's own picture; the turf is dark enough for the tufts as they were.
- **No islets yet** (HR5, as bobbing decals): an islet drawn into the floor would be ground no one can reach.
- **The whole island's screenshot** is taken through a viewport of its own on the game's world: Feel's party camera drives whichever camera the screen shows and never zooms out past 0.5.

## Holes

Holes are new to the game and general: the crypt's rooms may have them later (a pit, a broken floor). They are engine work in `core/` and `gameplay/`, built in their own steps (HR-H1 and HR-H2, below), and the hub's cracks are the first. Today a cell is either a wall (room walls, solid shots: walls to everything) or the ground of a body that cannot move (a prop's footprint: a wall to walking bodies only; shots pass); a hole is a third kind. Reviewed against the code by a fresh-context agent on 2026-11-16; its findings are written into the rules below.

1. **What a hole is.** A cell a walking body cannot step into, which nothing else minds: shots, beams, rays, sight and a portal's gates pass over it, and it is never cover. A room marks its holes as it marks its walls, by rectangles in the scene: a `StaticBody2D` whose `collision_layer` is exactly the hole layer (`RoomBase.HOLE_LAYER`; a body left on the default layer 1 would be a wall), with `RectangleShape2D`s (the hub's generator writes 8 px runs over every crack's cells into `hub_ground.tscn`). `RoomPlaces.hole_rects` reads them as `wall_rects` reads walls, and the room's size (`RoomPlaces._size_of`) counts them. Where a cell is both, the wall wins (a Wall card's stones over a crack are a wall). Flow hands the holes to the projectile manager with the walls (`_set_walls`) into a third grid over the same 8 px cells. **The hole grid is state, as the wall and ground grids are** (not DERIVED: `Flow.rebuild_derived` lays nothing again when the room is unchanged), cleared in `set_bounds` and `clear_walls`, and checked in the early exits of `slide_body` and `_way_hit`.
2. **Walking stops at the lip; the native kernel stays as it is.** `WallSlide.slide` already takes two grids, walls and ground. The projectile manager keeps a merged grid, ground and holes (kept up to date where footprints are laid and marked), and hands the kernel that one for a walk and plain ground for a move that crosses holes. `BodyKernel` and its byte-for-byte test do not change. **A body's step is slid in two parts** in a room with holes: first its dash and what is done to it (a push, pull, recoil, Plow's carry, by question 8), which cross holes, then its own walking, which does not. One slide of their sum could not tell them apart, and a body could walk into a hole while being pushed.
3. **Moves that cross:** a dash (the Mover's own), a teleport (it jumps; its back-off from walls ignores holes), a swap and a portal's exit (`target_move_requested`), and by question 8 knockback, a pull, recoil and a carry. **A teleport, a swap or a portal exit marks that tick as crossing**, since they are over at once in the caster's step, before the walk that would push the body out again.
4. **A body that ends over a hole falls.** Checked right after the body's move, every step: a body that walks (a Mover) whose centre lies on a hole cell, and that is crossing nothing this tick (no dash with time left, no push or carry left, no jump this tick), falls. Without that check, a wizard in a crack one or two cells wide would be pushed out of it by the next walk (a centre in a blocked cell leaves by its nearest open side), and a crack's narrow parts would never take anyone. **Outside the room's grid counts as a hole** in a room with holes, so a long dash off the edge falls too (question 9). **The fall is an actor state beside down** (`Actor`), so everything that asks whether a body is down (its hurt shape, casting, its orbits and beams, touches, the AI's targets, revives, the stone's count) treats a falling body the same: it stops, its moves end (answered, so a dash's `on expire` fires where it ended), it cannot be hit and casts nothing. Its state: the fall's seconds left (`HoleRules.FALL_SECONDS`, 0.6) and the last ground cell it stood on (kept each step; a cell with no hole beside it; reset when a room starts or the body is placed; none yet: the nearest player spawn), hashed. **Feel draws the fall from that state** (shrinking and sinking into the void, motes off it), not only from `body_fell`, so a machine that joins mid-fall draws it. Then, by question 10: a wizard comes back on its last ground cell, protected for a moment as a joiner is, in the hub at no cost; anything else that falls dies through Combat (its killer whoever last hit or moved it), so a room is still cleared. Sent on the bus: `body_fell(actor)`, `body_returned(actor, at)`.
5. **Edge cases, each with a rule.** A body downed over a hole falls (it would hover there, and a revive or the hub's stand-up would leave it on the hole). The boss never falls: its walk stops at the lip, and its own teleport, a swap or a portal that would set it over a hole leaves it at the nearest ground instead.
