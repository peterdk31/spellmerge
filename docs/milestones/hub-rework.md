# Hub rework: the Landing

**Status (2026-11-16): a draft for the owner.** Asked by the owner: "Can you come up with a complete rework of the hub? Right now it looks kinda rough. It should look cool and inviting." Nothing here is built. The steps are **HR1 to HR6** (HR for hub rework), one PR each with its tests, in order, once the owner has answered the questions at the end. It changes how the hub looks and where things stand in it, and adds one small general rule (a placed prop may start in a state of its own, for the campfire); otherwise never what the hub does: the stone, the altar, the lectern, the rack, the dummies, the prop yard, joining and the run's start all work as today (docs/milestones/online-lobby.md, items.md, run-generation.md, ground-cover.md).

## What is rough today

Seen in `tests/screenshots/shot_hub.gd`'s shots on main (2026-11-16):

1. **The floor is nearly black.** `art/tilesets/hub/floor.svg` is Outline (`#14121f`) with Stone shadow joints, almost the same colour as the void's own tile (`void.svg`), and the room's `ambient` (0.9) darkens it again, so the grass is the only thing that shows.
2. **You cannot see where the island ends.** The void and the floor are almost the same colour, so the edge is only the rim's embers; the "island floating in the void" does not read as an island. Its outline is a staircase of rectangles.
3. **Nothing is a place.** The stone, the lectern, the altar, the dummies and the rack each stand alone on the same dark bricks, with nothing round them saying what they are for or how to get there. The rack is six staffs lying in a row on the floor.
4. **No warmth.** The one light is the stone's, and only once everyone is on it. Nothing in the hub says "stay a while".

## The idea

**The Landing: where the dropped staffs land.** The canon already says a staff that loses a run "lands at the bottom of the shaft again" (docs/STORY.md, canon 7), and the hub's shape is open (STORY.md, "Not fixed yet"). So the hub becomes that spot: the shaft opens into a great dark hollow, and an island of turf and old stone hangs in it, with a column of pale light falling on it from the hole far above ("Home is up"). The staffs who landed here before made it a camp: a fire, a rack of staffs, a practice ring, a yard of the wizard's junk. The crypt's rooms lie below it, and the teleporter stone takes you down into them. **This moves the story:** today the intro (`gameplay/flow/intro.gd`) and canon 4 put the bottom of the shaft on the crypt's floor. With a yes to question 1, the intro's last shot lands on the Landing instead (its floor redrawn; HR5) and canon 4 says the shaft ends on the Landing, with the crypt under it.

The owner's picks stay: an island floating in the void, the rim of embers in the elements' colours (L1, "A, mixed elements"), the stone in the middle, the altar, the dummies together. The void stays dark, because a dark frame is what makes a warm, lit island look inviting.

## The rules

1. **The look only, but for the campfire.** The hub keeps its rules, its props, its events and its state. Every new piece is drawn: sprites, decals, `light_spot`s, `mote_source`s and Feel's view. The one new body is the fire pit (an existing prop, placed; rule 9). No new simulation member, so determinism, replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged; the content determinism and snapshot tests cover the hub as they do today.
2. **The scene is the one place a position is written** (content/rooms/README.md), so everything moved is moved in `hub.tscn`, and the owner can keep moving it in the editor.
3. **The island's shape is a file, its scene written from it.** Today the scene is "written from one island shape by a small generator (not in the repository)", so its floor bands, walls and rim must be kept in step by hand. The rework adds the shape as data (`content/rooms/hub/island.json`: the coastline as a smooth closed curve, its points and how rough it is, its seed; the islets the same) and the generator to `tools/` (`tools/hub_island.py`, with `tools/test_hub_island.py`), which draws the island's art from it per pixel (the turf, the lip, the underside, as PNGs cut in tiles of at most 1 MB) and works out its walls and rim, and writes them into a scene of their own, `content/rooms/hub/island.tscn` (the floor sprites, the wall rectangles and their shapes, the rim's points, the underside's sprites), instanced in `hub.tscn`. The generator never touches `hub.tscn`, so the owner can save it in the editor freely (the editor rewrites the whole file, so markers in it would not survive); everything placed by hand stays there. A test fails when `island.tscn` and the shape disagree. The underside's sprites lie over the void (wall), so they are not named `Floor*`, and `test_hub_data.gd`, which today reads only the room's direct `Floor*` children, reads them through the instance.
4. **Organic lines everywhere** (the owner, 2026-11-16). No straight edge and no tile-shaped edge anywhere the eye goes: the coastline, the clearings of earth, the slabs' broken rims, the paths and the fence all follow curves with noise in them, and the grass grows over the edges between them. The 32 grid stays where things stand and are sized, never in an outline. **The walls stay rectangles:** the game's walls are boxes only (`RoomPlaces.wall_rects` reads only `RectangleShape2D`, and the cover and the native body kernel take walls from it), so the generator covers the void with 8 px wall boxes merged into runs, a staircase of 8 px steps hidden under the drawn edge, the coast's art reaching a few pixels past the walls so no step shows. A wizard walking along the coast slides along steps of 8 px, which a later step may smooth if it feels rough (HR6). The rim's points follow the drawn coastline, not the steps.
5. **Static decoration is the room's own decals,** as the crypt's torches are (`content/rooms/crypt/decals/torch.tscn`): lanterns, the campfire's logs, the rack's frame, the fence, the islets, the beam, in `content/rooms/hub/decals/`, scenes of sprites (and an `AnimationPlayer` where something moves) with no script, each lantern a `light_spot` with its painted glow in `painted_light`. Decor is never a prop: every prop with no role would stand in the junk yard (`PropYard`).
6. **docs/STYLE.md is a starting point here, not a rule** (the owner, 2026-11-16: "The style guide is not necessarily to be followed"). The hub keeps the 32 grid and the pixel look, so it sits beside the crypt and the players, but it may go past the guide where that makes it look better: soft light and glow (the beam from above, the lanterns' and the fire's halos), colours and shades outside the palette (a warmer turf, a deep blue for the void), gradients in the void and the beam, and art drawn as PNGs instead of rect-only SVGs. Each step's screenshots are the check, not the style checklist. The hard rules (file types, sizes, licences, the manifest) still hold, and so does the code's own art test where it checks every SVG's colours (`tests/core/test_svg_art.gd`): art past the palette is a PNG, or the test learns which files may be. docs/STYLE.md gets a line saying the hub's art may go past it. The room's `ambient` goes back to 1.0.
7. **The grass's floor.** The cover's tufts (`content/cover/hub_grass/tufts.svg`) are Leaf shadow and base, so on a green floor their shadow pixels vanish (found drawing the mockups). With turf, the tufts are drawn again a shade lighter (Leaf base and light, or a shade of the hub's own) over a turf darker than them: see question 2.
8. **The seats' crystals use the player colours:** a crystal is a seat's marker, as a HUD panel's border is, so a player colour on screen still means a player. A crystal is lit while its seat is taken, from any machine: Feel hears `seat_joined` and `seat_left` (core/events), which every machine hears for every seat.
9. **A placed prop may start in a state of its own** (for the campfire; the one change to the simulation). `PropStandIn` gains `starts_in` (a state id of its prop; empty, its first, as today), apart from its `state`, which stays the editor's view only (no room sets one today). The game stands the prop in that state, not entered: no cue, no `entered` triggers, its look, `blocks` and `hittable` that state's, as a snapshot restores a prop. The state is the prop's own, hashed, so replays, snapshots and joining are unchanged; a prop yard's copy still starts in its first. `PropBase.problems()`-style checks name a `starts_in` its prop lacks (`test_room_places.gd`), and `tests/gameplay/flow/` tests a placed fire pit started lit, put out by frost and lit again.

## Steps

- **HR1: this plan,** and the owner's answers. *Needs the owner.*
- **HR2: the island.** The shape file, `tools/hub_island.py` and `island.tscn`; the turf, the ragged lip with its grass and pebbles, and the rock underside, drawn per pixel from the curve (rule 4); walls and rim written from the shape (the rim's final edge: HR5 does not move it again); `ambient` 1.0; the hub's grass drawn again for the turf (rule 7). `test_hub_data.gd` keeps every rule it has (every 8 px cell floor or wall and never both; the rim clockwise, the void on its left), reading the floor through the instance; the shape test in `tools/`. Screenshots: `shot_hub.gd`'s, and the whole island (a new shot with a camera of its own: the party camera never shows more than the room at zoom 0.5, `party_camera.gd`). *The biggest step; everything after it is placing.*
- **HR3: the plaza and the paths.** The plaza's ring floor with its broken rim, the lanterns, the winding paths (earth and stepping stones along curves, drawn by the generator from curves in the shape file), the stone and the lectern's zone placed. The cover's fields drawn again round the new places (`test_room_cover.gd`).
- **HR4: the places.** The landing (spawns, its light, its beam, `falling_dust`: `content/motes/`), the campfire (rule 9 and the fire pit placed), the rack, the training ring, the altar dais with the joiners' spawns, the junk yard's fence round `prop_yard`. `test_hub_data.gd`'s clearances (nothing starts in a wall, the yard clear of the stone, the altar and every spawn, the lectern clear) hold for the new places; its check that the room has exactly one `mote_source` (the rim) changes to finding the rim as the one line source, since the dust is a second source.
- **HR5: the void, the seats and the story.** The bobbing islets and a far layer of stars that moves slower than the island (a `Parallax2D` node, drawn only, no script; it replaces today's opaque `Void` sprite, which would hide it at z -3), the seats' crystals on the altar (Feel; `tests/gameplay/feel/test_altar_seats.gd`); with a yes to question 1, the intro's floor redrawn as the Landing and docs/STORY.md's canon 4 and 7.
- **HR6: the owner's playtest on `/preview/`,** and the fixes it asks for.

Each step updates the docs it touches: the hub's paragraph in content/rooms/README.md (the generator now in the repository, the new places, the cover's fields), content/cover/README.md (the hub's fields), content/motes/README.md (`falling_dust`), docs/STYLE.md (rule 6), docs/STORY.md (question 1), and docs/ROADMAP.md (an entry for this plan once the owner approves it).

## Questions for the owner

1. **The Landing:** the bottom of the shaft, the light from the tower above, a camp the dropped staffs made, with the crypt below it (canon 4 and the intro's last shot change to match)? Or keep it an island in the void with no story place, and the story as it is?
2. **The floor:** turf (Leaf shadow, a dark meadow, the grass over it) with stone only on the plaza, the paths and the slabs? Or stone everywhere with grass on it, as today but lighter?
3. **Brightness:** ambient 1.0 and warm lights everywhere (the mockups), with the void kept dark? Or keep it moody, lit only round the places?
4. **Size:** the mockups' island is about 44×28 tiles (today 40×26), so the places have room between them. Keep that, or fit it into today's size?
5. **The seats' crystals in the player colours** on the altar: yes, or a plain light?
6. **How far past the style guide:** the soft-light mockups (glow, a beam, a deep blue void), or the banded light of the first mockups?
7. **The plaza's runes lit with the stone:** worth making the plaza part of the stone prop (its look grows past its area, and the tests that hold a prop's layers to its area learn an exception)? Or leave the plaza plain room art, with only the stone itself lighting up, as today?
