## What the players see

- Rooms full of **things**: barrels, crates, braziers, crystals, urns, the hub's teleporter stone and altar. Almost all of them give off motes: embers off a brazier, glimmers off a crystal's facets, dust off a cracked urn, sparks running down a lit barrel's fuse.
- **Some block** wizards, enemies and shots, as walls do. Some can be walked over (a rune slab, the teleporter stone).
- **Some take hits** and **react**: fire on a barrel lights its fuse, and a second later it explodes and the blast hurts **everyone** nearby, wizards, enemies and other barrels, so barrels set each other off. Shooting a lit barrel makes it explode at once. Frost then fire cracks a stone urn. A crate breaks into rubble that no longer blocks.
- **Possess works on props.** A possessed barrel can't walk, but its buttons are its reactions: press, and it blows (taking the possession with it).

## Owner decisions (2026-11-10)

1. **A prop is an actor with no controller** (the owner, above). Health, the hurt circle, statuses, Combat's reactions, possession and casting are the kit's, with no second kind of body. What makes it cheap is general engine work (W2), not a separate table.
2. **A reaction casts spell cards, and the prop is the caster.** An explosion, a frost burst, a poison pool are cards (`blast` + `fire`), so combos and infusions work and chain reactions follow from the core rule: the payload's caster is the shot that fired it, here the prop (the owner's "one core rule, no special cases", 2026-10-27; docs/DECISIONS.md, "The caster kit is one implementation").
3. **A prop's spells are the world's:** a new team, `TEAM_WORLD`, that every team's shots hit and whose shots hit every team, other props too.
4. **Possess is kept** for props, by the one rule it has today: every hit possesses.
5. **One hit can fire several reactions** (the owner, unsure; the rule below makes it matter little).
6. **Props don't move in this plan.** No knockback on a prop, no rolling barrels. A prop with a Mover can come later.
7. **It runs beside the online lobby** ("Run beside it"), not after L2. W1 to W6 touch the actor kit, Combat and the wall grid, which the netcode stands on, so each of those PRs runs `tools/online_pair.sh` and the determinism tests before it merges, and rebases on whichever lobby PR merged first.
8. **Damage numbers are a node of their own, on every actor:** "make this a new node you can attach to an actor (since it is also useful on enemies/allies/bosses)", then "Add the damage numbers to every actor" (W5).
9. **The hub gets a place for every prop** ("This makes it easy for me to test them"): a prop yard that stands every prop there is, found by folder, in place of a gallery room in the sandbox (W8).

## The rule: if it acts it's an actor; if it only stands, it's a prop

A statue that shoots at you is an enemy (an actor with an AI controller and a statue look). A barrel, a crate, a brazier, the teleporter stone are props. A prop is still an actor, so nothing in Combat, Spells or possession needs to know the difference. The training dummy is already close to one: an actor with Health, a Hurtbox and a still AI.

## What a prop is made of

**One content folder each,** like a spell card: `content/props/<id>/` with `<id>.tres` (a `PropBase`), its art, `assets.json` and its own test `test_<id>.gd`. Deleting a prop deletes its folder. Every prop is the same shared scene, `content/props/base/prop.tscn` (an `Actor` with Health, Hurtbox, Caster, `PropStateTriggers` and `Footprint`, and no controller), filled in from its `PropBase`, so a new prop is data and art only.

**`PropBase`** (core/base/prop_base.gd):

- `health`, the hurt circle (`radius`, `offset`), `footprint` (cells it blocks, empty for none), `invincible` (the hub's)
- `staffs`: 1 to 4 staffs as an enemy's (`EnemyBase.staffs`), the cards its triggers and a possessor cast. A staff's wind-up is a fuse.
- `states`: a list of `PropState`, the first the one it starts in

**`PropState`:**

- `id` (`idle`, `lit`, `fusing`, `broken`)
- `triggers`: a list of `PropTrigger`, in order
- `blocks`, `hittable`: whether its footprint and its hurt circle are on in this state (rubble neither)
- `gone`: a state that frees the actor when entered (a crate that leaves nothing)
- the look: art layers, mote sources and a light spot (W7). View only.

**`PropTrigger`:** `when` (a trigger, below), its numbers, and what happens: `cast` (a staff of the prop's, by number, or none) and `then` (a state id, or none).

### Triggers (W6)

| `when` | Fires when | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| `element_hit` | a hit carrying that element lands on it | the element; at least this much damage (0: any) |
| `status` | a status is on it (a fire pool's burn) | the status |
| `reaction` | one of Combat's reactions between statuses happens on it | the reaction |
| `health_below` | its health falls below a share of its most | the share |
| `death` | its health runs out | |
| `entered` | its state was just entered | |
| `cast` | its staff N cast (by a trigger or by whoever possesses it) | the staff |

**Several triggers, one rule:** on a moment (a hit, a tick's statuses, a cast), **every** trigger of the **current state** that matches fires, in the order listed; the **first** of them that names a `then` sets the next state, once, after they all fired. Triggers belong to states, so "only once" is a state change, never a flag: a fusing barrel has no fire trigger, and a broken one none at all.

**Death doesn't free a prop by itself.** A prop whose health runs out runs its `death` triggers; it is freed only when it enters a `gone` state. Without a death row, its health stays empty and it stands (an invincible stone).

### The exploding barrel, written out

```
staffs   1: blast + fire, wind-up 1.0 s (the fuse)    2: blast + fire, no wind-up
idle     element_hit fire     → then fusing
         death                → cast 2, then broken          (shot apart: blows at once)
         cast 2               → then broken                  (possessed: press 2, it blows)
fusing   entered              → cast 1                       (the fuse burns, then it blows)
         death                → cast 2, then broken          (shot while lit: blows now)
         cast 1               → then broken
broken   (rubble: blocks off, hittable off, its own art and smoke motes)
```

A possessed barrel pressing 1 lights its own fuse the long way; pressing 2 blows at once. It needs no code of its own.

## W2: a body that waits costs little (engine, every actor)

General rules, true of any actor, never "if it is a prop":

- **A dormant body isn't stepped.** The Tick steps a body only when it has something to do: a controller, a Mover, a protection counting down, a staff winding up or winding down, a possession, a phase. `Actor.dormant()` says so; anything that wakes one (a hit, a status, a reaction's cast) wakes it for that tick on. A body with no Mover never moves (`move()` returns before the native call).
- **An untouched body hashes as one value** (`func idle() -> bool`, core/sim/README.md, "Made once, or waiting"): full health, its first state, no possession, its pools full, nothing winding up. Statuses live in Combat's rows and are hashed there.
- **Snapshots** (only if W1 says so): a room-placed body that is still idle is made again from the room, not from the snapshot; only the touched ones are saved.

**Tests:** `test_state_declared`, `test_state_walked`, `test_determinism` pass; a dormant body woken by a hit steps that tick and the next; a replay with props played twice agrees; W1's numbers again.

**Done (2026-11-11).** What was built differs from the bullets above; the first build differed again, and the owner turned it round:

- **First built: sleep with wakes.** Checking `dormant()` every tick cost as much as the step it saved (4.8 us a body), so a dormant body was put to sleep and everything that could give it work had to wake it: about nine `wake()` calls in six files, a check in every test for a missed one, and a test playing the game with sleeping on and off. The owner: "This seems like an awful lot of code to have to remember to call exactly at the right times? Think of another solution, please." It was taken out before merging.
- **Built: still bodies, a rule of the game.** A body that no controller drives and that has no Mover is **still** (`Actor.is_still`), and the Tick never steps it: one read of the actor table's `still`, which the body sets when its id opens and in `_tell_driver`, the one place a driver changes. Nothing has to wake it: a possession gives it a driver, and it is stepped while that lasts. What only a step does is not done for a still body, by rule: its pools do not refill, it takes no hit-stop hold (the table refuses it, and a hold ends when its body becomes still) and no protection (Health refuses it). No body of today's game is still: wizards, enemies, dummies and decoys all have a controller or a Mover.
- **Slowed wind-ups are Combat's** (the owner: "Move the wind-up slowing into Spells (it sounds like a good general move, but if it is not, dont do it!"). The Caster's step asked Combat for its pace and held its wind-ups back in Spells; Spells is in `core/` and may not call Combat, so Combat, which counts the statuses down, holds every slowed actor's wind-ups back in Spells in its own step (`Spells.hold_winds`), in the same tick and by the same arithmetic. The Caster no longer asks Combat anything, and a still prop's fuse is slowed by a chill too.
- **A resting body, not `idle()`.** `idle()` means a fresh object's state, and a snapshot makes an idle object again with `new()`, which would lose a body's place and data. So the hash alone sums a body up: `Actor.rest_leaf()` is a few values (where it stands and aims, its size, health, `invincible`, ticking, the staff on show), each hashed by its bytes, while the body is still, whole and every component `at_rest()` (Health full and unprotected, Hurtbox registered, Contact, Caster with its start cards unedited and every pool full, as a float32 holds it). These are questions a component answers about itself, false in the base: one that does not say is hashed whole. A snapshot still saves every body whole.

## W3: Spells' hash by caster (engine, every caster)

Added by the owner after W2 (2026-11-11): "the fix for this should be added to the plan as the next step". W2 found Spells' area of the hash growing by about 14 to 24 us a caster (every enemy's too): its installed staffs, its compiled spells by tag and its node counts are three tables over every caster, which the hash sorts and walks whole, and Spells is one area, so a spread hash pays all of it in one tick (15 to 28 ms at 1,000 props, by the machine's load).

- **A caster's part of Spells goes with its body.** Spells' per-caster state (what is kept by actor number: installed staffs, wind-ups, the spells by tag of that caster's builds, their node counts) is hashed as a part of its own, `Spells <id>`, which the spread hash (`SimHash.slice`) puts in the same part as that actor's body. Spells' own area keeps only what is no caster's (its carriers, moves, rays). A desync report names the caster.
- **A caster with nothing live is one value,** as a resting body is: its builds as installed (each a FROZEN compiled staff, already one leaf), nothing winding, no carrier of its alive, no node counted. The tables are walked only for the casters that have something going on.
- **No table is sorted to be hashed** where an index will do: a table keyed by tag or by caster is walked by caster in id order.
- The walker, the hash and snapshots stay generic: Spells says what its parts are (one method the hash asks, as `rest_leaf` is for a body), and a snapshot still saves Spells whole.

**Done when:** `prop_cost.gd` shows Spells' hash flat in the props (within a millisecond from 0 to 1,000) and the worst tick of a spread hash at 1,000 props under 5 ms; `test_state_walked`, `test_determinism`, `test_content_determinism`, the replays across platforms (`tools/platform_hashes.py`) and the desync report's tests pass; the stress run's hash numbers before and after are in docs/DECISIONS.md.

**Done (2026-11-11).** Built as above, with these differences:

- **Parts are a kind of member, not Spells' own code** (`BY_ACTOR`, core/sim/README.md, "The hash"): any area may list the tables it keeps by actor id, and hand each id's over (`actor_parts()`, `actor_part(id)`, empty for an id with none). Only `SimHash` makes parts and calls `actor_parts`/`actor_part` (`split_by_actor`, `slice`, `areas`; the desync report goes through `areas` with a sink): the area without those members under its own name, and "Spells 7" for caster 7, which `slice` puts with the body "actor 7"; an empty share is no area. The walker walks an area with or without its BY_ACTOR members, or a value it is handed, and knows nothing of parts. `SimHash.of`, a walk of the area itself and a snapshot take Spells whole, so nothing that hands Spells over alone loses a table.
- **What is parted:** the tables that grew with the casters (`_installed`, `_profiles`, `_node_counts`, `_live`, `_generation_live`, `_last_infused`). The packed arrays kept by actor (wind-ups, cooldowns, cursors, generations) stay in Spells' area: each is one engine `hash()` call whatever the number of casters (flat), and the desync report already names the first index that differs, which is the caster's row. `_penta_rings` (by a Penta card's tag) stays too: only casters of Penta have a row.
- **A resting caster** is its builds as installed and its last infusion (each FROZEN, one leaf). What says it rests is a new count, `_caster_live` (by caster, the sum of `_generation_live`, written only by the two functions that write that table; state, not DERIVED: exact in a snapshot with nothing to rebuild): at 0 its only generations are its staffs' current ones, whose spells are its builds' nodes (set and freed together), so the tables are not looked at. While something of its builds is alive, its part has one entry a generation it holds, staff by staff in order (looked up by key, never sorted): the generation, its node count, what is alive of it, its live shots by node and an older build's spells, so a report names a generation. **What the resting part stands on is checked** (`Spells.actor_part_problems()`): at every walk of every card (`test_state_walked.gd`), through a fight and a wrap of the generations, and a test changes each BY_ACTOR table in turn and fails unless its caster's part changes or `actor_part_problems` names it (a table with no case fails too).
- **The net check holds a body and a part an actor** (`NetMessage.MAX_AREAS` is twice the actor table and the services; it was 2,048, under 1,024 bodies and 1,024 parts).
- **The Tick keeps its bodies' names** ("actor 7", made once by id, not at every call): the spread hash asks for the areas every tick (`state_areas` took 0.66 ms at 1,000 bodies, most of it the names).

## W4: a prop in the game (engine)

- **`TEAM_WORLD`** in `core/actors`: every team's shots hit it and its shots hit every team. The AI never aims at the world team (it is nobody's foe); it only hits a prop by accident. A later AI step may teach enemies to shoot barrels near wizards.
- **`Footprint`** (`gameplay/actor/footprint.gd`): stamps the prop's cells into the wall grid (`ProjectileManager`, WALL_CELL 8 px, the way a solid shot does with `set_solid`) while its state `blocks`, and takes them out when it stops. Bodies slide along it as along any wall.
- **A shot that meets a prop hits the prop**, not a wall in front of it. Two ways, to settle in W4's code: the cells a prop stamps know their actor (a small map of prop cells beside the byte grid), so a shot ending in one is a hit on that prop; or the hurt circle covers the footprint and the target test runs before the wall test for those cells. Either keeps the native kernels' bit-for-bit twin (native/README.md).
- **Possess on a prop** works by today's rule; tests check it: the driver can't walk, its presses cast the prop's staffs, the prop's death ends the possession and the driver goes home.
- **A caster number for a prop:** its actor id, as for every actor (`Spells.open_caster`).

**Done (2026-11-11), after a turn with the owner.** The first build made a prop's footprint a wall for shots too (a hurt circle covering it, so a shot meeting it hit the prop first; a body's own spells let out of its own footprint, with a "whose spell" argument on every clip). The owner then decided: **"A shot should treat a prop like any other body"** (a piercing shot hits a prop and goes on), **props of any size**, and, asked whether walking actors should collide too, **one rule by whether a body can move**. So:

- **Aim's one rule, `Actors.is_foe(us, them)`:** another team is a foe, the world's never, and every team is the world's foe. Every aim test follows it: the projectile manager's homing, `nearest_target` and `targets_near` (the AI's target, a chain's jump, a zap, a decoy's aim; written out in those per-target loops), the native homing search (`TEAM_WORLD` among the constants it checks), Combat's touch (nobody touches a prop for damage), discharge and foes-only pull, and `enemy_died` (a foe of the wizards, by `Actor.own_team()`: a prop's death is no enemy's, possessed or not). Hits still land on everyone; shields, deflects and threats go by the shot's own team.
- **Every actor stands on a `Footprint`** (gameplay/actor/footprint.gd): a circle (`radius`, 6 by default) or a box (`size`), `offset` from the feet; its scene's, or one the actor adds itself as it adds `Phases`. The Mover's `body_radius` became it (the wizard 6, a mite 5, a husk 7, a decoy its own).
- **One rule, by whether a body can move:** a body that walks (a Mover) slides along the walls as its circle; a body that cannot move (no Mover: a prop, a training dummy, a rooted enemy later) is **solid ground** to the bodies that walk: it lays its footprint into the projectile manager's **ground grid** (`set_footprint(actor, centre, size, radius)`, a grid of its own over the wall grid's cells, counted exactly, laid again in a new grid) while it stands and `solid` (`set_solid`, W6's rubble). Bodies that walk still part softly (Combat's push). `WallSlide` and the native `BodyKernel` read both grids; `circle_blocked` too (a teleport does not land on a prop). Shots, rays, lines of sight and clipped segments never see the ground: a prop is hit by its hurt circle as any body, a plain shot ends on it, a piercing one goes on. A tree is hit on its canopy (its hurt shape) and blocks with its trunk (its footprint), and a wizard can stand behind the upper part of a prop (drawn over by its look, W7).
- **A body with no Mover never moves** (`Actor.move`), driven or not, **and takes no slam** (Combat): nothing ever spends its knock, so it would slam into the nearest wall after a heavy or gusting hit.
- **Possess:** by today's rule, tested with the real `possess` card (`test_possess_prop.gd`): the seat drives the prop but cannot walk it, its buttons cast the prop's staffs as the prop, the prop is on the seat's team while it lasts and the world's (and still) after, and its death ends the possession and the seat goes home.

**Tests:** `tests/core/test_projectiles_footprint.gd` (the ground grid), `tests/gameplay/actor/test_footprint.gd` (every actor's footprint, the one rule, a prop in play: pierce, own spells, the world team, touch, death, the slam), `test_possess_prop.gd`, `test_actors.gd` (`is_foe`), `test_wall_slide.gd` (both grids, native against GDScript), and two props of the world's team in `test_native_kernel.gd`'s scene (a native homing search without the world rule fails it at step 1).

**Reviewed** (an independent review of the first build, 2026-11-11): a heavy hit on a prop dealt a second hit (the slam rule above, kept); carriers clipped at a prop's own centre and inexact counts at a crowded cell (gone with the ground grid, which shots never see; its counts are exact); a call per target for `is_foe` (written out).

**Box hurt shapes (owner, 2026-11-11, before W6): done (2026-11-11).** Every hit shape was a circle, so a long prop (a bench) could not be hit where it is drawn. Built, general (a big boss can use it too):

- **One shape, one rule** (`HitShape`, core/projectiles/hit_shape.gd): a box `half` either way of its centre, rounded by a radius; a circle is the box of no size. What is within `r` of the shape is within `r` of its box rounded by its radius, so a circle and a box take every test the same way: how far a point is off it (`gap_sq`, `gap`), where a path comes nearest it (`along`: where it goes in, else the nearest end or corner, the first on a tie), how far a line passes off it (`line_gap_sq`), how wide it is seen from a point (`sides`), how far it reaches (`reach_toward`, `outer_radius`, `axis_reach`). For a circle each answer is the circle's old sum to the bit (`d.abs()` squares as `d`), so a world of circles plays as before (the recordings and every hash hold); the hot loops still test a circle their own way, for speed, and a box through these.
- **`Hurtbox.size`:** a box in place of the circle (`half()`, `is_box()`, `outer_radius()`); `Combat.register_actor(..., half)` keeps it and hands it to `add_target(..., half)`. `Actor.set_size` scales it.
- **Every test takes it:** a flying shot's hit test and its sparing of the body it started on, beams, sweeps (swings, blasts, slams, dashes: a box is seen from its corners), the area queries (`targets_in`, `targets_near`, `nearest_target`, `touches_target`: zaps, pulls, chains, discharge), homing (a box is in reach by its edge; `homing_target_reach`, was `homing_target_radius`, is how far it reaches toward the search, where a teleport lands), the grid's cell boxes and search widening (`_max_target_reach`), and Combat's touch (two shapes touch when one's centre is within both radii of the box of both halves). The native `ShotKernel` mirrors the hit test, the homing pick, the sparing and the cell boxes to the bit. What only asks how big a body is takes its corner (`outer_radius`): a zap's timing and a portal's fit (`targets_near`'s radii), a barrier's bubble, the AI's reach and dodging.
- **A footprint the actor adds under a box hurt shape is that box's base** (`Footprint.fit_base`): as wide, half as deep (`BASE_SHARE`, a guess to tune when the first props are drawn), its bottom edge on the box's. A scene's own footprint is kept as it is.

## W8a: a room is its scene (owner, 2026-11-11)

Added by the owner while reviewing W8, which had put the hub's yard, its stone's states and a layout of props into `RoomBase`: "Why was base_room edited with eg. beacon (hub specific) code? Lets discuss this", then "Are props not just Nodes placed in a room scene? I think we need to think carefully about what a room is and how it is designed", and "ideally I would like to be able to edit a room eg. the Hub by hand in the godot editor. This is easy if I can visually see what im moving." Then: "Write the plan and then implement it."

**What was wrong:** every position was written twice, in the room's `.tres` (`player_spawns`, `doors`, `dummy_spawns`, `start_stone`, ...) and as markers in its scene, with a test holding the two together, so moving a thing in the editor did nothing until the numbers were copied; and `RoomBase` gathered one room's rules (the hub's stone, altar, joiners, yard), which every room then carried.

**The rules:**

1. **The `.tres` says what kind of room it is:** `id`, `scene`, and rules over the whole room (`invincible`, `ambient`, the colour and mote overrides). No position, and nothing only one room has.
2. **The scene says what is where,** the one place a position is written, edited by hand in the Godot editor. Nothing in it runs (rooms carry no scripts), so everything placed is a plain node, its data in metadata, under a node named for what it holds:
   - `PlayerSpawns` (in seat order), `EnemySpawns`, `Doors`, `JoinerSpawns` (in seat order): `Marker2D`s, as today.
   - `Props`: each an instance of the prop's **stand-in**, `content/props/<id>/<id>_stand_in.tscn`: a `Sprite2D` drawing the prop's first picture where the game draws it, with `metadata/prop` its `PropBase`. Dragged in from the FileSystem dock, it shows in the editor what stands there; its position is the prop's feet.
   - `Dummies`: the same for an enemy that stands in the room (the training and shooting dummies): `content/enemies/<id>/<id>_stand_in.tscn`, `metadata/enemy` its `EnemyBase`.
   - `Zones`: `ReferenceRect`s (drawn in the editor only, moved and sized by their handles), each with `metadata/zone` a name (`start_stone`, `altar`, `prop_yard`).
3. **One reader:** `RoomPlaces` (core/base/room_places.gd) reads a room's scene into its places once (`RoomBase.places()`, kept): the spawns, doors, props and dummies with where they stand, and the zones by name; `problems()` names a stand-in with no data or a zone with no name. Core knows a zone only by its name.
4. **What a zone means is the game's:** the hub's rules (the stone lit while every wizard stands in `start_stone`, the props standing in it entering `lit`/`unlit`; `altar`; the yard, its layout and its 10 s return) live in gameplay/flow (`Flow`, `PropYard`), never in `RoomBase`.
5. **Flow makes the bodies,** at the run's start in the scene's order, as before (so actor ids, the hash and snapshots are as they were), and takes the stand-ins and zone rectangles out of the room it shows: they are the editor's.
6. **The editor never lies:** a test reads every room the registry knows and fails when a stand-in's picture or its offset is not what the game draws for its data, a stand-in has no data, or a zone has no name.

- **One stand-in for every prop:** `PropStandIn` (tools/prop_stand_in.gd) in place of a scene a prop: its `prop` picked in the Inspector, it draws that prop's look in the editor in any of its states (`state`, picked from the prop's own), as the game does (`PropLayer.dress`, which `PropLook` draws with too), with its hurt shape and ground outlined. `EnemyStandIn` (tools/enemy_stand_in.gd) shows an enemy's own scene. The `start_stone` and `altar` zones go; zones stay for places that are no thing (the prop yard).

## W8c: a room foundation (owner, 2026-11-11)

The owner asked for a review of W8a/W8b: "make sure that this is a good foundation for a room design system. In the future it should also be possible to place eg. grass and other visual (but maybe interactable when eg. walking on them/it?) nodes. The room system might also need to support 'automatically randomly generated rooms'. It should have low coupling and just be well designed in regards to good software architecture." An independent review found what blocked that; the owner: "Go with your recommendations, W8c in #700, build it."

**What the review found:** the room shown and the room read were different instances (`RoomBase.places()` cached its own), so a room built at run time (generated, put together from pieces) had nothing to read; `RoomPlaces` was a fixed list of holders found only under the scene's root, a new kind of placed thing meaning six edits, and props and dummies were two copies of one idea; the stone and altar were found by content id and state names written in Flow, stood on where their blocking footprint is, and `in_yard` put a hub idea into prop data; Flow (1,300 lines) held the room's contents, the yard and the hub's rules; a rotated holder would have put places through the engine's trigonometry (it differs between platforms); and the plan had drifted.

**The rules:**

1. **Built once, read from what is shown.** `RoomBase.build()` makes a room's layout (a fixed room: its scene; a generated room, M3: pieces put together by the run's seeded chance, its seed among the run's rules, so a joining machine builds the same room before a snapshot). Flow reads the places (`RoomPlaces.read`) and the size (`RoomPlaces.size_of`) from that instance, the one it shows. `RoomBase.places()` stays for tests and tools.
2. **One kind of placed thing, found anywhere.** A `RoomPlacement` (core/base, an editor script) is a point holding the data of what stands there (`placed()`); `PropStandIn` and `EnemyStandIn` (tools/) are two of them. A `RoomZone` (core/base, a `ReferenceRect`, drawn in the editor only) is an area with a name. Both are found at any depth, in the tree's order, so a scene piece carries its own; spawns and doors are markers under their holders (`PlayerSpawns`, ...) at any depth. Every placement and zone is taken out before play. A new kind of placed thing is a new placement class or data class, with no edit to the reader.
3. **Places are whole translations.** A position is the sum of positions up the tree (never through a rotation or a scale, which would go through the engine's trigonometry); a rotated, skewed or scaled node on the way is one of `problems()`.
4. **Bodies are the room's contents,** made in one loop (`RoomContents`, gameplay/flow): a placement's data makes its body by its class (a prop through `PropBody`, an enemy from its scene); it keeps the bodies standing, forgets one that goes in the tick it goes, and answers by role. Flow sequences runs and no longer stands bodies itself.
5. **A prop says what it is and where it is stood on:** `PropBase.role` (empty: nothing to the game's rules; the hub's `run_start` stone and `party_altar`) and `PropBase.area_size`/`area_offset` (where standing on it counts, apart from its blocking ground). The yard stands every prop with no role (`in_yard` goes).
6. **The hub's rules are their own object** (`HubRules`, gameplay/flow): they count the wizards on the `run_start` prop's area and the `party_altar`'s, by role, and light it by asking (`prop_state_requested`, a request on the bus its `PropStateTriggers` applies; no other area's component is called). A `run_start` prop has the states `lit` and `unlit`, checked by a test over every prop of that role.
7. **The yard keeps itself** (`PropYard`: its slots, its counts and its layout), stepped by Flow.

**Decoration, for later (conventions, nothing built until wanted):** static art is the scene's sprites and tiles, with `mote_source`/`light_spot` nodes (as today). View-only decor that reacts to bodies (grass swaying as a wizard walks through) is Feel's, never the simulation's: a decor field node in a group, scattered by Feel into one batch (`Batches.multimesh()`) and swayed by a shader fed the bodies near it, so its cost goes with the bodies, not the tufts. Decor that changes the game (grass that burns, slows or is trampled) is one simulation grid of cells (state, a service beside the ground grid), never an actor a tuft; it is a `RoomZone` with a data class of its own when it comes.

**Generated rooms, for M3:** a `RoomBase` whose `build()` puts pieces together by seeded chance; pieces carry their placements, zones and spawns (rule 2); the seed is in `SessionRules`.

**Done (2026-11-11), in #700.** Built as written: `RoomPlacement` and `RoomZone` (core/base, editor scripts), `RoomPlaces` reading at any depth with whole translations (`placed`, `placed_at`, `placed_of`, `of_kind`, `zone`, `size`), `RoomBase.build()`, `RoomContents`, `HubRules` and a `PropYard` that keeps its slots (gameplay/flow), `PropBase.role`/`area_size`/`area_offset`, `Events.prop_state_requested`. Flow went from 1,315 lines to about 1,130 and no longer stands bodies. Room size and walls are read from the built layout; the floor and wall colours a room's debris and marks take are still sampled from its scene (`RoomBase.surface_colors`, `floor_colors`): a generated room will give them by its biome. **Tests:** `tests/core/test_base.gd` (placements and spawns at any depth through a moved piece, by kind, a rotated or scaled piece named, every placement and zone stripped), `tests/content/rooms/test_room_places.gd` (every room's places sound, a stand-in drawn as its body, nothing left in the room shown), `tests/gameplay/actor/test_prop_state_triggers.gd` (a prop asked by the bus), `tests/content/props/test_prop_data.gd` (every role kept: a `run_start` has `lit`, `unlit` and an area), the flow tests on `RoomContents`, `HubRules` and `PropYard`.

## W8: the hub on props

The teleporter stone (states `unlit`, `lit`, nothing that blocks, no hurt circle: walked onto; since W8c a `run_start` prop stood on where its area is) and the altar move onto props; Flow sets the stone's state from `start_stone_changed`, by `prop_state_requested(actor, state)`, a request the prop's `PropStateTriggers` applies at its next step. The hub's rim stays a room's `MoteSource`. The hub looks the same or better, and `shot_hub` shows it.

**The prop yard:** a place on the island (the hub scene's zone `prop_yard` since W8a; first planned as `RoomBase.prop_yard`) where Flow stands **every prop the registry knows**, in rows by id, each in its first state, with room around them to try spells. A new prop's folder is all it takes for it to appear there; the hub is never edited for it. A yard prop that breaks or goes stands again, fresh, a few seconds later (`RoomBase.prop_yard_return`, 10 s), so testing never runs out of barrels. Wizards are invincible in the hub, so a blast still knocks them back and shows its numbers (W5) but takes no health. Yard props are the game's like any (hashed, in a joining machine's snapshot), so online players see the same barrels go up.

**Done (2026-11-11).** As built, with these differences:

- **Flow enters the stone's state itself** (`PropStateTriggers.enter`, made public for it in W7), in its own step where it counts the stone, so there is no `prop_state_requested`: the count and the state change are one tick's work in one place, the same on every machine, and a still prop needs no step to apply a request. Which prop is the stone was first where it stood (feet in a `start_stone` zone), then its id (W8b), and since W8c its role (`run_start`), which the hub's rules ask by the bus (`prop_state_requested`) to enter `lit` or `unlit` as the stone lights or goes out. Flow's own `stone_lit` cue and Feel's `start_stone_lit` group are gone: the cue is the lit look's, the look is the prop's, and a snapshot gives the state back with its look and no cue (`ActorLook.sync`).
- **The room places its props** in its scene (W8a, above: stand-ins under `Props`), in any mode. The hub's stone and altar are `content/props/hub_stone` and `hub_altar` (their art moved there from art/tilesets/hub/, with its manifest lines), both walked onto (`blocks` and `hittable` off) and invincible. A slab's feet are its middle, its picture's bottom 48 px below them (`PropLayer.offset`). (First built as `RoomBase.prop_ids`/`prop_spawns`; W8a replaced them before merging.)
- **A layer on the floor:** `PropLayer.drawn` (`AMONG_BODIES`, `OVER_BODIES`, `ON_FLOOR`, `HIDDEN`) replaces the two flags `over_bodies` and `hidden`; `ON_FLOOR` is the room floor's z (`RoomBase.FLOOR_Z`), under every body and the floor's marks, and may be the body's own layer.
- **The lit stone is a little more than before:** its runes' layer gives off ice glimmers (a mask), and it lights the floor round it (a light spot from the runes' colour); the cue is the same.
- **Not in the yard:** `PropBase.in_yard` (on by default) is off for the hub's stone and altar, which mean something only where the hub puts them. The yard stands the others by `PropYard.places` (gameplay/flow/prop_yard.gd): rows across the zone by each prop's `PropBase.extent` (its hurt shape and its ground), `PropYard.GAP` (32 px) apart, each row centred, its feet on one line; a prop that does not fit is left out with a warning. The hub's yard is `Rect2(320, 160, 640, 128)`, above the stone.
- **Fresh again:** a yard prop that leaves its first state (lit, broken) or goes is replaced `PropYard.RETURN_SECONDS` (10) later, where it stood. Flow forgets a body that goes in the tick it goes (it steps after Combat and the bodies), so its state never points at a freed one. The yard's slots, ids, places and counts are Flow's state (`_yard` a REFS list).
- **Tests stand props through their own registry:** `Registry.add(folder, resource)` registers a fixture as a scan would, on a registry of the test's own, since the game has no yard prop until W9 (`tests/gameplay/flow/test_flow_prop_yard.gd` stands two test exploding barrels).

## Not in this plan

- Props that move (knockback, rolling barrels), and a prop with a Mover walking while possessed.
- Loot from props (a trigger may only cast cards and change state; drops are M3's).
- An enemy AI that uses props on purpose.
- Props placed by a room generator (M3); here a room's scene stands its props (W8a: stand-ins under `Props`), and pieces of scene a generator puts together would carry theirs.
- Health bars on props: a hit flashes and shows its damage number (W5); a state may show cracks (`health_below`).
- A gallery room in the sandbox: the hub's prop yard is the place to try props (owner, 2026-11-10).
