# AI

## Purpose

Shared enemy behaviours: steering, targeting and attack patterns.

Built across M1 to M3 (the plan gives the range, not one milestone; see "Gameplay sub-areas" in docs/PLAN.md).

Enemies in `content/enemies/` pick and configure behaviours from here instead of writing their own: an enemy is an actor (gameplay/actor) driven by the AI here and drawn by a look from here. Targeting chooses among every actor of another team, never "the player". The decoys and familiars of the staffs' cards live here too (staffs B5).

## Contract

- **Public entry scripts:** `gameplay/ai/ai.gd` (`AIController`), `creature_look.gd` (`CreatureLook`), `dummy_look.gd` (`DummyLook`) and the autoload `Entities` (`gameplay/ai/entities.gd`). Other code uses only these and the events they send and listen to; nothing else in the folder is public.
- **Enemies are actors (actors G6, docs/milestones/M2-actors.md):** an enemy scene's root is an `Actor` (gameplay/actor) with the kit's components: Health, Hurtbox (an imp's 11 px at (0, -12)), Contact, Mover and Caster, which the actor fills from its `data` (the enemy's `EnemyBase`: health, walking speed, contact damage, staff, cards, mana); the spawner sets `data` before adding it. Every enemy body has an actor id (the enemies' team, the enemy ramp, no seat) that owns its hurt circle, closed when it leaves the tree. The body walks, casts, is hurt, pushed and slowed by its components; this area only decides and draws.
- **`AIController` (`ai.gd`)** is the enemy's home controller (an `ActorController` child of the actor): it writes the actor's intent and nothing else. It targets the nearest actor of another team than the actor's that can be hit now (`Projectiles.nearest_target` with the team: a downed wizard has no hurt circle, so it is skipped), a wizard's decoy within 240 px first (`Entities.lure`); it retargets every `RETARGET_TIME` (0.5 s, staggered), at once when the target is lost or downed (`player_downed`), and once at the first step. **Grudges (infighting):** hurt by an actor of its own team (`damage_dealt` naming the actor it drives, with the attacker's actor id), it targets that actor for `GRUDGE_TIME` (3 s), then goes back to its team's targets; a wizard's hit and its own blast earn none. States: CHASE (walk to the target: `intent.move`, aim at it), WINDUP (in `attack_range`, only when `Caster.cast_ready(windup_time)`, else it holds; stand, aim locked), the cast (`intent.press(0)` at the end of the windup: the Caster casts staff 0 from the staff tip along the aim and pays its mana), RECOVER (stand, aim still locked). The windup and recovery timers slow by `Combat.pace` (the Mover slows the walk by the same), so a frozen enemy's attack waits. An actor with no staff only chases, into its target, and hurts by touch (Contact). `is_telegraphing()`, `telegraph_progress()`, `target`, `grudge`, `grudge_left()`, `state`; exports `attack_range`, `windup_time`, `recover_time`. `started(actor)` starts a fresh chase and `stopped(actor)` forgets it (a possession hands the actor to another controller with `Actor.drive`, G9). It names its home actor to `Entities` (`set_enemy`, by actor id) and ends its decoys and familiars when it leaves the tree (`enemy_gone`, before the actor's caster closes). All timing runs in `think(actor, delta)`, called by `Actor.step`, so tests drive actors with fixed deltas.
- **`CreatureLook`** (an `ActorLook`) draws an enemy: the idle, windup and hurt poses on the scene's `Sprite` (`windup_texture`, a strip of 32 px frames that flips faster toward the cast and holds its bright frame for the last 20%; `hurt_texture`, shown 0.25 s after `damage_dealt` names the actor (a burn tick that does not kill sends none), over the windup too; the `AnimationPlayer` pauses meanwhile), the flash (`modulate`) and the `Telegraph`: a `Sprite2D` with `gameplay/ai/telegraph.gdshader` (the shot look in its own small shader: the shot shader's cost per pixel was too much for a crowd winding up at once), its ramp (`ramp_base`, `ramp_light`) set in the scene and its material local to the scene. While the AI driving the actor winds up, the look sets `beam_length` and the region to the next cast's reach (`Caster.next_cast_reach`), `telegraph_lane` (the next cast's radius plus a margin) and `telegraph_progress`, turned to the aim; a cast that is not aimed shows only the pose and flash, and an actor no AI drives shows no telegraph. `is_hurt()`, `telegraph_reach()`. All of an enemy's drawing is here, none in the AI. Its nodes are exports (`sprite`, `telegraph`, `animation`), else the actor's children of those names.
- **`DummyLook`** shows what the sandbox's training dummy took (an actor with Health, a Hurtbox, no controller and no Mover, on the enemies' team by `idle_team`): damage numbers per `damage_dealt`, damage per second over the last 5 s from its health in Combat (burn ticks count), its statuses and the last reaction at its body; `dps()`, `status_text()`, `shown_numbers()`, `total_damage`.
- `Entities` (staffs B5) keeps the decoys and familiars cards make, in a fixed pool (four of each kind per player; one more ends the oldest). A decoy is a still copy of its caster's wizard, drawn by the shot shader in its player's colour, part fallen apart, with a glow (never see-through; the glow look, 2026-10-27), that every shot but its own wizard's hits (a `core/projectiles` target on `LAYER_PLAYER` owned by its player) and that enemies within 240 px chase before any wizard (`lure`, asked by `ai.gd`); it breaks after three hits or when its time runs out. A familiar (drawn as an orbit shot by the shot shader: a small white-hot core in its player's colours and a tail bending round its circle) circles its caster (`entity_spawn_requested`'s `caster`: its wizard; the shot whose trigger made it, followed while it flies; or the point it was made at, once that shot is gone) and, every second, asks for a cast at the nearest enemy within 200 px. `seconds` of INF (or 0 or less) lasts until `entity_end_requested`. A new room or the end of the run clears them all without `entity_ended`. `step(delta)` does the work. **Entities by actor (actors G3b):** a spawn belongs to its `caster`, an actor id; an owner a seat drives uses the players' rows, any other the enemies' (until G8). **Enemies' entities (F8d):** a spawn from an enemy's actor belongs to that enemy. Its decoy copies the enemy, in the enemy ramp, and is a target on `LAYER_ENEMY` owned by its actor: every shot but its own enemy's hits it (three break it), wizards' homing and familiars pick it, and enemies never chase it (`lure` is for wizards' decoys only). Its familiar circles the enemy and casts at the nearest body of another team within 200 px (a wizard or a wizard's decoy), under its actor id. Each enemy keeps two of a kind; enemies share 32 rows of their own after the players' 32, so a crowd never takes a wizard's (full, the oldest enemy entity ends). The AI names its body for its actor when it opens it (`set_enemy`) and, as it closes it, ends its entities where they are with `entity_ended` (`enemy_gone`), so even a `hold` one goes with the enemy.
- **Staffs (staffs F8a, docs/milestones/M2-staffs.md "Enemy staffs"):** an enemy whose data has a `staff` and `cards` casts them like a wizard: its Caster (gameplay/actor) installs them as staff 0 under its actor id (compiled once per staff and cards for every caster), gets mana back (`mana_regen`, plus siphon's `mana_refund_requested`), and casts what the AI presses. Caster-side cards (F8c: dash, teleport, hook, swap, recoil) move it through its Mover, one implementation for every actor. Decoys and familiars (F8d): see `Entities` above. **Everything hurts everything (actors G3c):** an enemy's spells hit the other enemies too, and its blasts hurt it; teams only aim.
- **Dependency direction:** `core` ← `gameplay` ← `content`. This area may use `core/` and the registry. `core/` never depends on it, and content never reaches into it.
- **Events** (`core/events/events.gd` owns the definitions; this list matches it and the table in [gameplay/README.md](../README.md)):
  - Sends: `entity_ended` (a decoy broke or ran out, a familiar ended; for the end point) and `familiar_cast_requested` (Entities). An enemy's casts, moves and hurts are its actor components' (gameplay/actor: `caster_move_finished` from its Mover; Combat sends `damage_dealt` for its spells' hits).
  - Listens to: `entity_spawn_requested`, `entity_end_requested`, `projectile_hit` (a shot on a decoy), `room_entered` and `run_ended` (Entities); `player_downed` (an AI whose target is down retargets at once) and `damage_dealt` (a hit on the actor an AI drives, for grudges) (`AIController`); `damage_dealt` (`CreatureLook`: the hurt pose); `damage_dealt` and `reaction_triggered` (`DummyLook`). The actor's components hear the rest (`caster_move_requested`, `target_move_requested`, `mana_refund_requested`, ...; see gameplay/actor/README.md).
- Areas never call each other directly; they talk through events. [gameplay/README.md](../README.md) has the rules every sub-area shares.

## Allowed files

`.gd` and `.tres`, plus `.uid`, `.import` and `.license` files. Limit: 1 MB per file. Typed GDScript only; no banned APIs. The text shared by all sub-areas is in [gameplay/README.md](../README.md).

## Example to copy

`gameplay/ai/ai.gd` (`AIController`) and `creature_look.gd` are the example, put together in the enemy `content/enemies/ember_imp/` (`ember_imp.tscn`: the actor and its components; `ember_imp.tres`: its numbers and staff). Its tests show how to test it: `tests/gameplay/ai/ai_fixture.gd` builds a world (its own bus, actor table, `Combat`, projectile manager and `Spells` with the real cards from a scanned registry, up to four wizards) and imps from the real scene, in the tree and stepped by hand (`Actor.step(delta)` with fixed deltas, moving the body by `velocity` as `move_and_slide()` would); `test_ai.gd`, `test_ai_telegraph.gd` and `test_ai_actors.gd` (ids, an enemy with no staff, grudges) use it.

## Review questions

A good PR here adds or improves one reusable behaviour that several enemies can configure, targets any of the 1–4 players, and stays cheap with 100–200 enemies. Besides the general review:

- Are behaviours reusable by several enemies? Do 100–200 enemies stay within the frame budget?
- Does it keep to the dependency direction, without calling another area directly?
