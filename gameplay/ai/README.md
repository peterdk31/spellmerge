# AI

## Purpose

Shared enemy behaviours: one AI that plays whatever staffs an actor holds (docs/milestones/M2-enemy-ai.md), steering, targeting and the looks.

Built across M1 to M3 (the plan gives the range, not one milestone; see "Gameplay sub-areas" in docs/PLAN.md).

Enemies in `content/enemies/` pick and configure behaviours from here instead of writing their own: an enemy is an actor (gameplay/actor) driven by the AI here and drawn by a look from here. Targeting chooses among every actor of another team, never "the player". The decoys and familiars of the staffs' cards live here too (staffs B5).

## Contract

- **Public entry scripts:** `gameplay/ai/ai.gd` (`AIController`), `brain.gd` (`AIBrain`), `behaviour.gd` (`AIBehaviour`), `behaviours.gd` (`AIBehaviours`), `team_board.gd` (`TeamBoard`), `sight.gd` (`AISight`), `creature_look.gd` (`CreatureLook`), `dummy_look.gd` (`DummyLook`) and the autoload `Entities` (`gameplay/ai/entities.gd`). Other code uses only these and the events they send and listen to; nothing else in the folder is public.
- **Enemies are actors (actors G6, docs/milestones/M2-actors.md):** an enemy scene's root is an `Actor` (gameplay/actor) with the kit's components: Health, Hurtbox (an imp's 11 px at (0, -12)), Contact, Mover and Caster, which the actor fills from its `data` (the enemy's `EnemyBase`: health, walking speed, contact damage, staff, cards, mana); the spawner sets `data` before adding it. Every enemy body has an actor id (the enemies' team, the enemy ramp, no seat) that owns its hurt circle, closed when it leaves the tree. The body walks, casts, is hurt, pushed and slowed by its components; this area only decides and draws.
- **`AISight` (`sight.gd`, owner 2026-11-01):** what an AI sees of walls, shared by the enemy AI and the test bots: `line_clear(projectiles, from, to)` (no room wall and no Wall card's solid shot on the segment: the projectile manager's wall grid, which shots stop on; `Projectiles.line_clear` walks the whole segment), `detour(projectiles, from, goal, probe)` (of eight ways whose first `probe` pixels are free, the one that brings `goal` into view, else the free one ending nearest it) and `cover(projectiles, from, threat, probe)` (of the eight free ways, one whose end has a wall between it and `threat`, the farthest from it; ZERO when none is near). Static, nothing allocated. It asks nothing about what a wall is or who made it, and nothing about what an AI's own staffs cast (owner: which staff to fire needs more design). Test: `tests/gameplay/ai/test_ai_sight.gd`.
- **`AIController` (`ai.gd`)** is the enemy's home controller (an `ActorController` child of the actor): it writes the actor's intent and nothing else, by its `brain` (an `AIBrain`). Its profile is `profile` when set, else its home actor's data's (`EnemyBase.profile_or_fallback()`). It hears the driven actor's own `Actor.damaged` (grudges, guarding) and its Caster's `staff_built` (decide again at once); nothing listens on the bus per enemy. `state`, `target`, `grudge`, `grudge_left()`, `lookup_count`, `windup_time` and `recover_time` (the attack under way, else the next one of its telegraph staff), `is_telegraphing()`, `telegraph_progress()`, `telegraph_staff()`, `carry_grudge()`; `State` is `AIBrain.State`, and `RETARGET_TIME`, `GRUDGE_TIME`, `SIGHT`, `CLOSE_ENOUGH` name the brain's for the horde swarm. A minion needs no code of its own: an `AIController` of the players' team handed the actor (`Actor.drive`) makes it fight the other enemies (`test_ai_staff.gd`); an enemy's AI that possesses a wizard plays the wizard's four staffs. `started(actor)` starts a fresh chase and `stopped(actor)` forgets it. It names its home actor to `Entities` (`set_enemy`, by actor id) and ends its decoys and familiars when it leaves the tree (`enemy_gone`, before the actor's caster closes). All timing runs in `think(actor, delta)`, called by `Actor.step`, so tests drive actors with fixed deltas.
- **`AIBrain` (`brain.gd`, enemy AI AI5, docs/milestones/M2-enemy-ai.md):** the AI every enemy, minion, boss and test bot plays by. It **perceives** on its profile's `reaction` timer (staggered; at once after an attack or a new target): the target (the nearest standing actor of another team, `Projectiles.nearest_target`; a player's decoy within 240 px first, `Entities.lure`; a teammate that hurt it for `GRUDGE_TIME`, 3 s), the distance and whether the line is clear (`AISight`), the soonest shot coming (`Projectiles.threat`, only with `dodge` > 0), its health, and **what each staff casts next** (`Caster.next_cast_sense`, a `CastSense`; never a card id, `tests/core/test_ai_names_no_card.gd`), which are ready within their windup and which hold a sustained cast (left alone: a press would switch it off). The **range band** comes from its harmful casts' reach (`band_min` = reach × (0.75 − 0.6 × aggression), at least past its own blast; `band_max` = reach × 0.9): a bolt makes a kiter, a swing a rusher. It **decides** by asking every behaviour (`AIBehaviours.all()`) how much it `want`s to act (the running one gets `commitment` × 0.3; `randomness` sometimes takes the second best) and lets the winner `act`, which writes the plan (`plan_staff`, `plan_move`: STAND, TOWARD, AWAY, STRAFE or WAY with `plan_way`, `plan_urgent`). It **carries it out** in `state`: CHASE (walk as planned, aim at the target; wind up the planned staff once `Caster.cast_ready(windup)`, else wait), WINDUP (stand, aim locked with lead and a seeded aim error of up to `AIM_ERROR` × (1 − accuracy)), the cast (`intent.press`), CHANNEL (a cast that `repeats_held`: hold the button while the target stays in its lane, the mana lasts and `channel_max` is not up, turning at `track_speed`), RECOVER (stand; a shot about to hit may cut it short), REPOSITION (walk `AISight.detour` for `REPOSITION_TIME` or until the line is clear) and TRANSITION (a boss's phase change). Windups grow with danger: `WINDUP_BASE` + `WINDUP_PER_DAMAGE` × damage (+ `WINDUP_AREA` for an area or blast), clamped, × `windup_scale`; harmless casts `WINDUP_HARMLESS`; rests `REST_BASE` + half the delay, × `rest_scale`; both in whole sixteenths of a second (`TIME_GRAIN`). An urgent plan presses a staff whose next cast moves the caster once, with no windup, and the move goes the way it walks. Timers slow by `Combat.pace`. Nothing allocates per step. Tests: `test_ai_brain.gd`, `test_ai_combos.gd` (every combo file played by an enemy: cast, never into a wall, never killing itself; the heal-only combo waits for a heal behaviour).
- **Behaviours (`behaviour.gd`, `AIBehaviour`; `behaviours/`; the list `behaviours.gd`, `AIBehaviours`):** each is one stateless file (`name()`, `want(brain)`, `act(brain)`), one copy shared by every AI; per-AI memory goes in the brain's `picks` and `memo` at the behaviour's `index`. **A new behaviour is one file and one line in `behaviours.gd`.** Now: `chase` (0.1; the only one for an actor with nothing that hurts), `keep range` (`position.gd`: out of the band, in or back), `fight` (the ready staff that hurts and reaches, along a clear line, never a blast that would catch itself; less keen too close for an aimed cast; waits for a staff in reach that is not ready), `flank` (in reach but walled off: REPOSITION), `close in` (far, with a move ready: cast it at the target), `dodge` (`dodge` × urgency: off the shot's line, urgent when it is near), `guard` (hurt or under fire, a `blocks` cast ready), `evade` (low health or crowded: to cover, else away, urgent), `open` (a `spawns` or `sustained` cast once, before anything else, while its last has run out).
- **Phases:** `AIController.phase_started` makes the brain stand (`AIBrain.transition`) through a boss's phase change and take the phase's profile; `CreatureLook` swaps the body sprite to the phase look's (`Phases.started`). The phases themselves are the actor kit's (`gameplay/actor/phases.gd`).
- **`TeamBoard` (`team_board.gd`, enemy AI AI6):** what a team's AIs share, one for every team, kept and stepped by `Entities` (`board`; cleared on a new room). `started(team, who)` at each windup; `beat(team, who, teamwork)` makes a teammate's attack a little less wanted for `BEAT` seconds after (never its own), so a barrage reads as a sequence.
- **`CreatureLook`** (an `ActorLook`) draws an enemy: the idle, windup and hurt poses on the scene's `Sprite` (`windup_texture`, a strip of 32 px frames that flips faster toward the cast and holds its bright frame for the last 20%; `hurt_texture`, shown 0.25 s after a hit on the actor (its `Actor.damaged`; a burn tick that does not kill sends none), over the windup too; the `AnimationPlayer` pauses meanwhile), the flash (`modulate`) and the `Telegraph`: a `Sprite2D` with `gameplay/ai/telegraph.gdshader` (the shot look in its own small shader, as every shot shader has its own kind: core/README.md, "The shot shaders"), its ramp (`ramp_base`, `ramp_light`) set in the scene and its material local to the scene. While the AI driving the actor winds up, the look sets `beam_length` and the region to the next cast's reach of the staff it winds up (`ActorController.telegraph_staff()`, `Caster.next_cast_reach`), `telegraph_lane` (the next cast's radius plus a margin) and `telegraph_progress`, turned to the aim; a cast that is not aimed shows only the pose and flash, and an actor no AI drives shows no telegraph. `is_hurt()`, `telegraph_reach()`. The poses are optional: the strays (living staffs H5) have none, since their `Face` carries the mood (angry at rest, furious while the controller `is_telegraphing`, worried when hit). **The hop:** a stray walking faster than `HOP_SPEED` hops `HOP_HEIGHT` px `HOP_RATE` times a second on the sprite's `offset` (the telegraph and hurt circle stay put), and bobs `HOP_IDLE` px standing; the actor's `Face` (stepped after this look) gets the same lift as its `body_offset`; `lift()`. On top it plays its cast's staff animation (ActorLook's `pose_offset` and `apply_pose`: the carrier card's `staff_pose`, so a stray's rain raises its staff glowing as a wizard's does). Players float, so the two sides read apart by motion. A step sets nothing on the nodes that did not change (the pose, the whole-pixel lift). All of an enemy's drawing is here, none in the AI. Its nodes are exports (`sprite`, `telegraph`, `animation`), else the actor's children of those names.
- **`DummyLook`** shows what the sandbox's training dummy took (an actor with Health, a Hurtbox, no controller and no Mover, on the enemies' team by `idle_team`): damage numbers per `damage_dealt`, damage per second over the last 5 s from its health in Combat (burn ticks count), its statuses and the last reaction at its body; `dps()`, `status_text()`, `shown_numbers()`, `total_damage`.
- `Entities` (staffs B5) keeps the decoys and familiars cards make, in a fixed pool (four of each kind per player; one more ends the oldest). A decoy is a still copy of its caster's body, drawn by `core/projectiles/shot_sprite.gdshader` in its player's colour, part fallen apart, with a glow (never see-through; the glow look, 2026-10-27), that every shot but its own body's hits (a `core/projectiles` target on `LAYER_PLAYER` owned by its player) and that enemies within 240 px chase before any player (`lure`, asked by `ai.gd`); it breaks after three hits or when its time runs out. A familiar (drawn as an orbit shot by `core/projectiles/shot_orbit.gdshader`: a small white-hot core in its player's colours and a tail bending round its circle) circles its caster (`entity_spawn_requested`'s `caster`: its player; the shot whose trigger made it, followed while it flies; or the point it was made at, once that shot is gone), starting on the side where its card put it; it casts nothing itself (core/spells fires what its triggers give it, rules R4 F3). `seconds` of INF (or 0 or less) lasts until `entity_end_requested`. A new room or the end of the run clears them all without `entity_ended`. `step(delta)` does the work. **Entities by actor (actors G3b):** a spawn belongs to its `caster`, an actor id; an owner a seat drives uses the players' rows, any other the enemies' (seated or not, `Actors.seat_of`). **G8:** the caster's body (what a decoy copies and a familiar circles) is the node the actor table knows (`Actors.node_of`), or the body the AI named (`set_enemy`); no group or player index is read. **Enemies' entities (F8d):** a spawn from an enemy's actor belongs to that enemy. Its decoy copies the enemy, in the enemy ramp, and is a target on `LAYER_ENEMY` owned by its actor: every shot but its own enemy's hits it (three break it; a decoy whose card has a shield or deflect turns the other teams' shots away first, through `CarrierDecoy.of(Spells).guard_decoy` every step, rules R4g), players' homing picks it, and enemies never chase it (`lure` is for players' decoys only). Its familiar circles the enemy. Each enemy keeps two of a kind; enemies share 32 rows of their own after the players' 32, so a crowd never takes a player's (full, the oldest enemy entity ends). The AI names its body for its actor when it opens it (`set_enemy`) and, as it closes it, ends its entities where they are with `entity_ended` (`enemy_gone`), so even a `hold` one goes with the enemy.
- **Staffs (staffs F8a, docs/milestones/M2-staffs.md "Enemy staffs"):** an enemy whose data has a `staff` and `cards` casts them like a player: its Caster (gameplay/actor) installs them as staff 0 under its actor id (compiled once per staff and cards for every caster), gets mana back (its staff's `mana_regen`: the pool is the staff's, `StaffBase.max_mana`; plus siphon's `mana_refund_requested`), and casts what the AI presses. Caster-side cards (F8c: dash, teleport, pull, swap, recoil) move it through its Mover, one implementation for every actor. Decoys and familiars (F8d): see `Entities` above. **Everything hurts everything (actors G3c):** an enemy's spells hit the other enemies too, and its blasts hurt it; teams only aim.
- **Dependency direction:** `core` ← `gameplay` ← `content`. This area may use `core/` and the registry. `core/` never depends on it, and content never reaches into it.
- **Events** (`core/events/events.gd` owns the definitions; this list matches it and the table in [gameplay/README.md](../README.md)):
  - Sends: `entity_ended` (a decoy broke or ran out, a familiar ended; for the end point; Entities). An enemy's casts, moves and hurts are its actor components' (gameplay/actor: `caster_move_finished` from its Mover; Combat sends `damage_dealt` for its spells' hits).
  - Listens to: `entity_spawn_requested`, `entity_end_requested`, `projectile_hit` (a shot on a decoy), `room_entered` and `run_ended` (Entities); `damage_dealt` and `reaction_triggered` (`DummyLook`, the one sandbox dummy). No enemy listens on the bus per actor (performance pass, 2026-10-28: such a listener was called for every hit on anyone): `AIController` (grudges) and `CreatureLook` (the hurt pose) hear the driven actor's own `Actor.damaged`, and an AI notices a downed target by itself at its next step. The actor's components hear the rest (`caster_move_requested`, `target_move_requested`, `mana_refund_requested`, ...; see gameplay/actor/README.md).
- Areas never call each other directly; they talk through events. [gameplay/README.md](../README.md) has the rules every sub-area shares.

## Allowed files

`.gd` and `.tres`, plus `.uid`, `.import` and `.license` files. Limit: 1 MB per file. Typed GDScript only; no banned APIs. The text shared by all sub-areas is in [gameplay/README.md](../README.md).

## Example to copy

`gameplay/ai/ai.gd` (`AIController`) and `creature_look.gd` are the example, put together in the enemy `content/enemies/ember_imp/` (`ember_imp.tscn`: the actor and its components; `ember_imp.tres`: its numbers and staff). Its tests show how to test it: `tests/gameplay/ai/ai_fixture.gd` builds a world (its own bus, actor table, `Combat`, projectile manager and `Spells` with the real cards from a scanned registry, up to four players) and imps from the real scene, in the tree and stepped by hand (`Actor.step(delta)` with fixed deltas, moving the body by `velocity` as `move_and_slide()` would); `test_ai.gd`, `test_ai_telegraph.gd` and `test_ai_actors.gd` (ids, an enemy with no staff, grudges) use it.

## Review questions

A good PR here adds or improves one reusable behaviour that several enemies can configure, targets any of the 1–4 players, and stays cheap with 100–200 enemies. Besides the general review:

- Are behaviours reusable by several enemies? Do 100–200 enemies stay within the frame budget?
- Does it keep to the dependency direction, without calling another area directly?
